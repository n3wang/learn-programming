> **Track:** Network CCNA — fourteenth chapter. Classic STP (thirteenth) can take **50 s**. This chapter is **Rapid PVST+**: same tree, faster ports. Exam **2.5**. Do not learn the RSTP **sync handshake** — costs, states, roles, link types, and the extra toolkit.

# Rapid Spanning Tree Protocol

**802.1D** waits on timers. A silent root port can sit **20 s** (max age) plus **15+15** before the spare forwards. That was fine once. It is not fine now.

**RSTP** (**802.1w**) keeps the **same algorithm** and talks neighbors into **forwarding now**. Cisco’s version is **Rapid PVST+**: one tree **per VLAN**, like PVST+ was to 802.1D.

## Versions

| Version | Whose | Trees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STP (802.1D) | IEEE | One for the LAN |
| PVST+ | Cisco | One per VLAN |
| RSTP (802.1w) | IEEE | One for the LAN |
| Rapid PVST+ | Cisco | One per VLAN |
| MSTP (802.1s) | IEEE | You **group** VLANs into instances; uses RSTP speed |

Cisco boxes run **PVST+** or **Rapid PVST+**, not the bare IEEE protocols. Default depends on model and IOS.

```text
SW1# show spanning-tree
VLAN0001
  Spanning tree enabled protocol ieee
```

**`ieee` = PVST+.** Configure Rapid PVST+ with `spanning-tree mode rapid-pvst`. The same line then says **`rstp`**. `spanning-tree mode pvst` puts **`ieee`** back.

Per-VLAN trees cost CPU: 100 VLANs → 100 instances and 100 BPDUs out every designated port every 2 s. In real life you often want **two** trees (switch A root for half the VLANs, switch B for the rest). **MSTP** puts many VLANs in one instance. Know that sentence; do not configure MSTP here.

## Same algorithm, different leftovers

Root, root port, designated port: **identical** to the last chapter (lowest BID; cost / neighbor BID / neighbor port ID; switch root cost / BID). Extra hub tiebreak — **lowest local port ID** — was already true for classic STP.

Leftover ports: STP said **nondesignated**. RSTP says **alternate** or **backup**.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-2.png" alt="The same tree under RSTP: the non-designated ports are now called alternate ports." caption="Figure 15.2 — The same tree under RSTP: the non-designated ports are now called alternate ports." maxWidth={560} />

**BPDUs:** classic STP — only the **root** originates; others **forward** on designated ports. RSTP — **every** switch sends on its designated ports every **2 s**, even if the root went quiet.

## Short and long costs

Classic “short” costs stop at 10 Gbps = **2**. RSTP added **long** costs up to 10 Tbps.

| Speed | Short | Long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 Mbps | 100 | 2,000,000 |
| 100 Mbps | 19 | 200,000 |
| 1 Gbps | 4 | 20,000 |
| 10 Gbps | 2 | 2,000 |
| 100 Gbps | — | 200 |
| 1 Tbps | — | 20 |
| 10 Tbps | — | 2 |

Anchor **10 Gbps = 2,000**. Ten times the speed → one-tenth the cost.

A Rapid box may still use **short** by default. `show spanning-tree pathcost method`. `spanning-tree pathcost method` with `short` or `long`.

## States

RSTP folds STP **blocking + listening** (and disabled) into **discarding**. Then **learning**, then **forwarding**.

A new port starts **discarding**. Alternate/backup stay there. Root/designated go to **forwarding** two ways:

- **Sync works** (RSTP neighbor, full duplex) → **FWD now**.
- **Sync fails** (PC, router, classic STP switch) → discarding 15 s, learning 15 s, then FWD — classic timers.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-3.png" alt="RSTP port states: discarding, then straight to forwarding if sync succeeds, or learning first if it fails." caption="Figure 15.3 — RSTP port states: discarding, then straight to forwarding if sync succeeds, or learning first if it fails." maxWidth={420} />

RSTP and STP can share a cable. That port on the Rapid side **behaves like STP**. `show spanning-tree` still prints **BLK** for discarding.

PortFast still jumps a host port to FWD. Use it.

## Roles: alternate and backup

**Root** and **designated** are unchanged. **Designated bridge** = the switch that owns the designated port on a segment (both STP and RSTP).

**Alternate** — spare path **toward the root**. Not root, not designated, and this switch is **not** the designated bridge. Picture: discarding port facing a **designated port on another switch**. Ready to FWD if the root port dies.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-4.png" alt="Alternate ports: neither root nor designated, on a switch that isn’t the segment’s designated bridge." caption="Figure 15.4 — Alternate ports: neither root nor designated, on a switch that isn’t the segment’s designated bridge." maxWidth={560} />

**Backup** — second path onto the **same segment** as a designated port **on this switch**. That means a **hub** (one collision domain, two of your ports). Not root, not designated, and this switch **is** the designated bridge. Picture: discarding port facing a designated port **on the same switch**. You will almost never see one. Exam still might.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-5.png" alt="Two ports on one shared segment: SW1’s becomes alternate, SW3’s spare becomes a backup port." caption="Figure 15.5 — Two ports on one shared segment: SW1’s becomes alternate, SW3’s spare becomes a backup port." maxWidth={560} />

On a hub, lowest **local** port ID wins DP or RP. The root bridge is the designated bridge for **every** segment — not “every port on the root is designated” if two root ports share a hub.

## Topology change: 6 s, not 50

Classic: wait **max age 20** after BPDUs stop (link still up), then 30 s of LIS+LRN.

RSTP: wait **three missed hellos** (**6 s**), then sync → FWD. Port **down/down** skips the 6 s — often **under 1 s**.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-6.png" alt="RSTP after a silent failure: about 6 seconds to reconverge instead of 50." caption="Figure 15.6 — RSTP after a silent failure: about 6 seconds to reconverge instead of 50." maxWidth={480} />

Do not memorize the TC flood. Remember: RSTP fires TC when a **non-edge** port goes **forwarding**. Edge up/down is not a topology change. Classic STP fired on almost any FWD/BLK transition.

## Link types

- **Point-to-point** — **full duplex**. Sync allowed. Auto from duplex. Or `spanning-tree link-type point-to-point`.
- **Shared** — **half duplex** (hub). No sync; 30 s walk. Auto, or `link-type shared`. Rare.
- **Edge** — host + **PortFast**. FWD now without sync. **Not** `link-type`. `spanning-tree portfast` (or `portfast default` on access). No PortFast → a PC still waits 30 s.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-7.png" alt="RSTP link types: point-to-point between switches, shared on a hub, edge toward hosts." caption="Figure 15.7 — RSTP link types: point-to-point between switches, shared on a hub, edge toward hosts." maxWidth={480} />

`show spanning-tree` Type column: **P2p**, **Shr**, **P2p Edge** (full + PortFast) or **Shr Edge** (half + PortFast).

Between switches: P2p. To a PC: P2p Edge.

## Root Guard, Loop Guard, BPDU Filter

PortFast and BPDU Guard were last chapter. These three work on **STP and RSTP**.

**Root Guard** — stop a foreign switch with a **better BID** from becoming root (provider vs customer). `spanning-tree guard root`. Superior BPDU → **root-inconsistent** (`BKN *ROOT_Inc`). Customer raises its priority; superior BPDUs stop; port **recovers itself**.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-8.png" alt="Root Guard on the provider’s ports blocks a customer switch advertising a better (superior) BPDU." caption="Figure 15.8 — Root Guard on the provider’s ports blocks a customer switch advertising a better (superior) BPDU." maxWidth={600} />

**Loop Guard** — an alternate that **stops hearing BPDUs** (neighbor software fault) must **not** become designated/FWD and close a loop. `spanning-tree guard loop`. → **loop-inconsistent** (`BKN *LOOP_Inc`). BPDUs return → recover.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-15-9.png" alt="Without Loop Guard: SW2 stops sending BPDUs, SW3’s alternate port starts forwarding, and a loop forms." caption="Figure 15.9 — Without Loop Guard: SW2 stops sending BPDUs, SW3’s alternate port starts forwarding, and a loop forms." maxWidth={560} />

Root Guard and Loop Guard **cannot share a port**. One reacts to **superior** BPDUs; the other to **missing** BPDUs.

**BPDU Filter** — usually a bad idea.

- Interface `spanning-tree bpdufilter enable`: **no send, ignore receive**. STP is **off**. A second switch can loop the LAN.
- Global `spanning-tree portfast bpdufilter default`: PortFast ports **stop sending**. A **received** BPDU turns **PortFast and Filter off**; the port becomes a normal STP/RSTP port.

Prefer **BPDU Guard** on host ports. Filter is not Guard.

## Exam shapes

- `ieee` vs `rstp` in `show spanning-tree`. `mode pvst` / `rapid-pvst`.
- MSTP = group VLANs, RSTP speed.
- Same algorithm. All designated ports send BPDUs. Short vs long costs (10G long = 2,000).
- Discarding = BLK+LIS. Alternate vs backup. Local port-ID hub tiebreak.
- 3 × hello = 6 s. Edge ≠ TC.
- P2p / shared / edge (PortFast).
- Root Guard vs Loop Guard vs interface Filter (STP off).
