This chapter is the two Cisco helpers that sit on top: **DTP** (who becomes a trunk) and **VTP** (who copies the VLAN list). Exam **2.1**, **2.2**. They are not named on the blueprint; you still configure them every time you touch a Cisco switch. Spanning Tree is the thirteenth chapter.

# DTP and VTP

**Dynamic Trunking Protocol (DTP)** decides whether a Cisco switch port **operates** as access or trunk. **VLAN Trunking Protocol (VTP)** copies the **VLAN database** (`vlan.dat` in flash) among Cisco switches in one **domain**.

Both are **Cisco-proprietary**. A non-Cisco switch will not speak them. Pin **`switchport mode trunk`** toward another vendor, and do not expect VTP to create VLANs there.

## Administrative vs operational

**Administrative mode** is what you typed with `switchport mode`. **Operational mode** is access or trunk after the two ends have talked.

If you pin the mode, they match:

- `switchport mode access` → always **access**. The port does **not** send DTP.
- `switchport mode trunk` → always **trunk**. The port **still sends DTP**, so a dynamic neighbor can join the trunk.

`show interfaces g0/0 switchport` prints both lines, plus `Negotiation of Trunking: On|Off`.

A router has no DTP. **ROAS** needs a **manual** trunk on the switch. Same rule toward any non-Cisco switch.

## DTP negotiation

Two more modes tell the port to **use DTP** instead of pinning:

- **`dynamic auto`** — default on Cisco ports. Willing to trunk, will **not** start it.
- **`dynamic desirable`** — willing, and **asks**.

Until both sides agree, a dynamic port **operates as access**.

```text
SW1# show interfaces g0/0 switchport
Administrative Mode: dynamic auto
Operational Mode: static access
```

**Static access** means the VLAN is the one in `switchport access vlan` (or VLAN 1). Dynamic access (VLAN from the connected host) is out of scope.

**auto + auto = access.** Two factory Cisco switches, cabled, stay access. That surprises people who expected a trunk.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-1.png" alt="Two ports left at dynamic auto exchange DTP and settle on access." caption="Figure 13.1 — Two ports left at dynamic auto exchange DTP and settle on access." maxWidth={480} />

Change SW1 to **desirable**. Auto on SW2 agrees. Operational mode is **trunk**.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-2.png" alt="Dynamic desirable on one side and dynamic auto on the other: DTP forms a trunk." caption="Figure 13.2 — Dynamic desirable on one side and dynamic auto on the other: DTP forms a trunk." maxWidth={480} />

Memorize the matrix. **access + trunk is invalid** — do not use it. The switches may detect the mismatch and block, or only the access VLAN and the trunk’s **native** VLAN (both untagged) will pass.

|  | access | trunk | desirable | auto |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **access** | access | invalid | access | access |
| **trunk** | invalid | trunk | trunk | trunk |
| **desirable** | access | trunk | trunk | trunk |
| **auto** | access | trunk | trunk | access |

Who sends DTP? **access: no. trunk / auto / desirable: yes.**

Old boxes that still have **ISL** can also negotiate encapsulation (`switchport trunk encapsulation negotiate`). Two **negotiate** sides pick **ISL**. One side **dot1q** or **isl**, the other **negotiate**, follows. A mismatch is a misconfiguration. Modern Cisco is **802.1Q only** — set `dot1q` if the command exists, as in the VLAN chapter. Do not expect ISL questions.

## Disable DTP

DTP was meant to save typing. It is also a hole: a host that **speaks DTP** can turn a **dynamic auto** port into a **trunk** and see every VLAN. End hosts normally send no DTP, so auto facing a PC stays access — until someone forges the messages. Do not leave that door open.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-3.png" alt="An attacker speaks DTP to a dynamic port, forms a trunk, and reaches every VLAN." caption="Figure 13.3 — An attacker speaks DTP to a dynamic port, forms a trunk, and reaches every VLAN." maxWidth={480} />

Two ways to silence it:

1. `switchport mode access` — access sends no DTP. `Negotiation of Trunking: Off`.
2. `switchport nonegotiate` — never send DTP, **even on a trunk**.

Pin **trunk**, then **nonegotiate**, when the far end is already a trunk (router, other vendor, or another Cisco you pinned). Best practice: **always** pin the mode **and** `nonegotiate`, so a later `mode trunk` does not start talking DTP again.

```text
SW1(config)# interface g0/0
SW1(config-if)# switchport mode access
SW1(config-if)# switchport nonegotiate
```

## VTP copies vlan.dat

`show vlan brief` is the VLAN database. The file is **`vlan.dat`** in flash (`dir flash:` / `show flash:`). If SW2 never learned VLAN 4, a tagged frame for VLAN 4 arriving on a trunk is **dropped**. Small lab: type `vlan 4` on SW2. Dozens of switches: that typing is how you create **drift**.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-4.png" alt="SW2 drops PC1’s frame because VLAN 4 is missing from its VLAN database." caption="Figure 13.4 — SW2 drops PC1’s frame because VLAN 4 is missing from its VLAN database." maxWidth={560} />

VTP floods VLAN **create / delete / rename** to other Cisco switches in the same **domain**, out **trunks only**, not access ports.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-5.png" alt="SW1 creates VLANs 2–4; the other switches in domain Manning sync and copy its revision number." caption="Figure 13.5 — SW1 creates VLANs 2–4; the other switches in domain Manning sync and copy its revision number." maxWidth={560} />

A **revision number** starts at **0** and increments by **1** per database change. A switch **syncs** only to a **higher** revision in the **same domain**. Equal or lower is old news.

Factory domain name is **blank** — domain **NULL**. You can still create VLANs; you **do not advertise**. `vtp domain Manning` on one switch is enough: other NULLs **adopt** the name from the first VTP message they hear on a trunk.

```text
SW1(config)# vtp domain Manning
Changing VTP domain name from NULL to Manning
SW1(config)# vlan 2
SW1(config-vlan)# vlan 3
SW1(config-vlan)# vlan 4
```

`show vtp status` then shows domain **Manning**, **8** existing VLANs (1, 2–4, plus reserved **1002–1005**), **Configuration Revision : 3**. Neighbors that joined the domain match that revision.

VLANs **1002–1005** cannot be removed (Token Ring / FDDI leftovers from the VLAN chapter). Ignore them except when the “existing VLANs” count jumps from 5 to 8.

## VTP modes

`vtp mode` is **server** (default), **client**, **transparent**, or **off**.

- **Server** — create / rename / delete. Advertises. Syncs to a higher revision.
- **Client** — **cannot** `vlan N`. Otherwise like a server. IOS: `VTP VLAN configuration not allowed when device is in CLIENT mode.`
- **Transparent** — own database, **revision always 0**, does **not** advertise or sync. **Forwards** VTP for the rest of the domain. Use it when this switch must be managed alone without breaking the others.
- **Off** — own database, no advertise, no sync, **no forward**. If the LAN is not using VTP, `vtp mode off` on every switch.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-6.png" alt="SW2 in transparent mode passes VTP messages along but keeps its own VLAN database." caption="Figure 13.6 — SW2 in transparent mode passes VTP messages along but keeps its own VLAN database." maxWidth={560} />

Cisco’s current advice: leave capable switches as **servers**. Memory is not the 1990s problem. You do not need a client tier for the exam topology.

## Versions, primary server, extended range

`show vtp status` lists **Version capable : 1 to 3** and **version running : 1** (default). `vtp version 3` is the one you want if you run VTP at all.

v1 and v2 are close. v2’s extra is Token Ring — skip it.

**Off mode** arrived in **version 3**. Boxes that can run v3 can still use **off** while running v1 or v2. Before that, **transparent on everyone** was the only “mostly off.”

**Version 3 primary server.** Only **one** switch may edit VLANs. Other servers are **secondary** — same as clients until you promote them. `vtp primary` is **privileged EXEC**, not global config (`do vtp primary` from `(config)#`). A second `vtp primary` demotes the first. That is how v3 stops a lab switch from overwriting the LAN.

**Extended-range VLANs (1006–4094)** propagate only in **version 3**. v1/v2 servers handle **normal range 1–1005**. The old workaround was transparent, which also left the domain.

## The revision trap

v1/v2 sync to the **highest revision in the domain**, whoever sent it. A lab switch at revision **50**, same domain, trunked into a LAN at revision **5**, replaces VLANs **10 / 20 / 30** with whatever the lab still has. Hosts in the vanished VLANs go dark.

<LessonFigure src="/img/ccna/fig-13-7.png" alt="A newly connected SW4 with a higher revision number wipes out everyone else’s VLANs." caption="Figure 13.7 — A newly connected SW4 with a higher revision number wipes out everyone else’s VLANs." maxWidth={600} />

Reset revision to **0** **before** the first production trunk:

1. Change the **domain name** to something else, then **back** (all versions).
2. `vtp mode transparent`, then back to server or client (**v1/v2 only**).
3. `vtp mode off`, then back to server or client (**v1/v2 only**).

Version 3 does not sync to a random newcomer — only to the **primary**. That is the reason v3 exists for this exam.

## Exam shapes

- Table: auto+auto access, desirable+anything-but-access trunk, access+trunk invalid.
- `show interfaces g0/0 switchport`: admin vs operational vs **Negotiation of Trunking**.
- Best practice: **mode** + **`nonegotiate`**.
- `show vtp status`: NULL, revision, mode, version. Client cannot `vlan`. Transparent rev **0**, still forwards.
- v3: **`vtp primary`** in **enable**, extended VLANs, revision bomb is gone.
- Reset rev 0: domain bounce; or (v1/v2) transparent / off bounce.
