# 12. Repeated Games: Cooperation, Trigger Strategies, and the Folk Theorem

> **Teaching rewrite** — follows the topic outline of MIT 14.12 lecture notes ch. 12 (MIT OpenCourseWare). Payoffs and examples are original.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. **Explain** why cooperation unravels in a finitely repeated game whose stage game has a unique equilibrium.
2. **Compute** present values and average payoffs with a discount factor δ.
3. **Describe** strategies in repeated games as plans that depend on histories (grim trigger, tit-for-tat).
4. **Use** the single-deviation principle to find the δ above which cooperation is sustainable.
5. **State** the folk theorem: feasible, individually rational payoffs, and the minmax.

## 12.1 Finitely repeated games

Play the Prisoners' Dilemma below a known number of times $T$.

<GameMatrixLab preset="pdRepeated" />

In the **last** round there is no future, so both defect. In round $T - 1$, nothing done now affects round $T$'s play, so both defect again… By backward induction, **both defect in every round**.

> **Theorem.** If the stage game has a **unique** subgame-perfect (Nash) equilibrium, then the finitely repeated game has a unique SPE: play the stage equilibrium every round, whatever happened before.

Cooperation needs a future that can **reward** good behaviour or **punish** bad behaviour. (With several stage equilibria, finite repetition *can* support cooperation by threatening to switch to a worse one at the end.)

## 12.2 Infinitely repeated games with observed actions

Now the game continues forever (or, equivalently, ends each round with some probability, so no round is known to be the last). Payoffs are discounted by $\delta \in (0, 1)$ per round.

**Present value** of a stream $u_0, u_1, u_2, \dots$: $\sum_{t \ge 0} \delta^t u_t$. A constant stream $u$ is worth $u/(1 - \delta)$. The **average payoff** $(1 - \delta) \sum_t \delta^t u_t$ puts streams on the same scale as stage payoffs.

A **history** is the list of all past actions; a **strategy** says what to do after every history. Two classics:

- **Grim trigger:** cooperate as long as everyone has always cooperated; after any defection, defect forever.
- **Tit-for-tat:** cooperate first; then copy the opponent's last move.

### Is grim trigger an SPE? The single-deviation test

Stage payoffs: reward $R = 5$ (both cooperate), temptation $T = 7$, sucker $S = 0$, punishment $P = 1$.

- **After a defection** (punishment phase): both defect forever — the stage equilibrium repeated, so no single deviation helps.
- **On the cooperative path:** cooperate forever gives $R/(1 - \delta)$; deviating once gives $T$ now and $P$ forever after: $T + \delta P/(1 - \delta)$.

$$
\frac{R}{1 - \delta} \ge T + \frac{\delta P}{1 - \delta} \iff \delta \ge \frac{T - R}{T - P} = \frac{7 - 5}{7 - 1} = \frac13.
$$

Try *Grim, but cheat in round 5* against *Grim trigger*: below δ = 1/3 the cheater's present value is higher; above it, lower. Try tit-for-tat against a cheater: punishment is short and forgiving.

## 12.3 The folk theorem

Which average payoffs can repeated play sustain?

- **Feasible payoffs:** any weighted average of stage-game payoff vectors (the convex hull — achievable by alternating outcomes or using public randomisation).
- **Minmax payoff** of player $i$: the lowest payoff others can hold $i$ to when $i$ best-responds. In the Prisoners' Dilemma it is $P = 1$ (others defect; your best response is to defect too).
- **Individually rational:** each player gets more than her minmax.

> **Folk theorem.** Any feasible payoff vector that gives every player strictly more than her minmax payoff can be achieved as the average payoff of a subgame-perfect equilibrium, provided players are patient enough (δ close to 1).

The green region in the lab's picture is everything above (1, 1) and inside the feasible set. Repeated interaction makes **almost any** cooperative arrangement self-enforcing — which is good news for long-term business relationships and bad news for prediction: the theory says cooperation is *possible*, not that it will happen.

## Key takeaways

- **Finite repetition** of a game with a unique equilibrium cannot sustain cooperation: it unravels from the end.
- **Infinite (or uncertain-end) repetition** + patience: trigger strategies make cooperation self-enforcing when $\delta \ge (T - R)/(T - P)$.
- Check SPE with the **single-deviation principle**: compare one deviation + the continuation with sticking to the plan.
- **Folk theorem**: any feasible, individually rational payoff can be sustained with enough patience.
