# 13. Implicit Cartels: Tacit Collusion and Price Wars

> **Teaching rewrite** — follows the topic outline of MIT 14.12 lecture notes ch. 13 (MIT OpenCourseWare). Numbers are original; demand is normalised so that $a - c = 1$.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. **Model** tacit collusion as a subgame-perfect equilibrium of a repeated Cournot or Bertrand game.
2. **Derive** the critical discount factor for monopoly collusion with Nash-reversion trigger strategies (9/17 for a Cournot duopoly).
3. **Explain** why collusion is harder with more firms and with price rather than quantity competition.
4. **Find** the most profitable sustainable output when firms are not patient enough for full monopoly.
5. **Explain** price wars: harsh short punishments (carrot and stick) and wars triggered by unobservable demand shocks.

---

## 13.1 Repeated Cournot oligopoly

Firms cannot sign enforceable cartel agreements (they are illegal), but they interact **repeatedly**. Inverse demand is $P = 1 - Q$ with zero cost (so $a - c = 1$).

**Simple trigger strategy:** each firm produces its share of the monopoly output, $q_m = 1/(2n)$, as long as everyone has; after any deviation, everyone produces the Cournot quantity $1/(n + 1)$ forever.

By the single-deviation principle we only need to check deviations **on the cartel path** (the punishment phase repeats a stage Nash equilibrium, which is always credible).

For the **duopoly**:

| | Per-period profit |
|---|---|
| Cartel (each produces 1/4) | $\pi_c = \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac18$ |
| Best cheat against 1/4: produce $(1 - \tfrac14)/2 = \tfrac38$ | $\pi_d = \left(\tfrac38\right)^2 = \tfrac{9}{64}$ |
| Cournot (punishment): each produces 1/3 | $\pi_N = \tfrac19$ |

Collusion is sustainable iff $\dfrac{\pi_c}{1 - \delta} \ge \pi_d + \dfrac{\delta\,\pi_N}{1 - \delta}$, i.e.

$$
\delta \ge \frac{\pi_d - \pi_c}{\pi_d - \pi_N} = \frac{9/64 - 8/64}{9/64 - 1/9} = \frac{9}{17} \approx 0.53.
$$

## 13.2 More firms, prices instead of quantities

- With $n$ Cournot firms, each firm's slice of the monopoly profit shrinks while the one-shot gain from cheating does not shrink as fast — the threshold rises toward 1.
- With **Bertrand** competition, a cheater undercuts slightly and takes the **whole** market for one period; punishment drives profit to 0. Collusion requires $\tfrac1n \cdot \tfrac{\pi_m}{1 - \delta} \ge \pi_m$, i.e. $\delta \ge 1 - 1/n$.

Antitrust authorities therefore worry most about markets with **few firms, frequent interaction, transparent prices** (so cheating is detected fast — effectively a high δ), and **stable demand**.

---

## 13.3 When firms are not patient enough

If δ is below the threshold, the monopoly outcome cannot be sustained — but firms may still sustain **some** collusion: a symmetric output $q$ between monopoly and Cournot. The best sustainable $q$ is the **lowest** output (highest price) satisfying

$$
\pi_c(q) \ge (1 - \delta)\,\pi_d(q) + \delta\,\pi_N.
$$

The lab finds it numerically: slide δ below 9/17 in the Cournot duopoly and watch the cartel's output move from 1/4 toward 1/3. Collusion is a matter of degree.

---

## 13.4 Price wars

Nash reversion is not the only — or the harshest — punishment.

- **Carrot and stick.** After a deviation, firms flood the market for **one or a few periods** (a price war harsher than Cournot), then return to collusion. A more severe punishment deters cheating at lower δ; returning to cooperation makes the punishment itself credible (the punishers also want to get back to the cartel).
- **Unobservable demand.** If firms see only the market price, not rivals' outputs, a low price could mean cheating **or** a bad demand shock. A collusive scheme must respond to low prices with temporary price wars even though nobody cheated — so price wars occur **on the equilibrium path**, typically in downturns.

Historical cartels — railway rate agreements, OPEC quota systems, vitamin and chemical cartels — show exactly these patterns: long collusive stretches punctuated by price wars.

## Key takeaways

- Tacit collusion = an SPE of the repeated game sustained by the threat of future competition.
- Cournot duopoly with Nash reversion: monopoly collusion iff $\delta \ge 9/17$. Bertrand: $\delta \ge 1 - 1/n$.
- **Few firms, frequent and transparent interaction, stable demand** favour collusion.
- Below the threshold, firms can still sustain partial collusion.
- **Price wars** arise as short, harsh punishments — and, with imperfect monitoring, even without any cheating.
