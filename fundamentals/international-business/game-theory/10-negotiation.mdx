# 10. Negotiation: Agendas, Settlements, and Bargaining

> **Teaching rewrite** — follows the topic outline of MIT 14.12 lecture notes ch. 10 (MIT OpenCourseWare). Models are simplified; stories and numbers are original.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. **Apply** backward induction to voting over a binary agenda and explain **sophisticated (strategic) voting**.
2. **Explain** agenda-setting power: who controls the order of votes can control the outcome.
3. **Solve** a pre-trial settlement negotiation and show why cases settle immediately and who captures the saved legal costs.
4. **Solve** finite alternating-offer bargaining by backward induction and explain the roles of **patience** and the **last word**.

## 10.1 Voting on a binary agenda

Legislatures rarely vote on everything at once. A bill, an amendment, and the status quo are voted on **in pairs**: for example, first *Bill vs Amendment*, then the winner vs the *Status quo*. Each legislator has strict preferences, and everything is common knowledge.

**Sincere voting** means voting for whichever option you like more at each stage. **Sophisticated voting** applies backward induction: in the last vote everyone votes sincerely, so each first-stage option is really a vote for **what it will lead to**.

<AgendaVotingLab />

With the default preferences:

- Final stage: if *Bill* wins round 1, it beats the status quo (Ana and Ben prefer it); if *Amendment* wins round 1, it **loses** to the status quo (Ana and Caro prefer the status quo).
- So round 1 is really "Bill vs Status quo". **Ben**, whose favourite is the Amendment, votes for the Bill in round 1 — the Amendment would only lead to the status quo, his worst outcome.
- Sincere voting gives the status quo; sophisticated voting gives the Bill.

Now change *which alternative is voted on last* — with identical preferences, the agenda setter can often pick the winner.

## 10.2 Pre-trial negotiations

A plaintiff sues a defendant. If they go to court, the judge awards $J$ to the plaintiff, but each side pays an extra court cost ($C_P$, $C_D$). Before the trial date they alternate settlement offers; every day of negotiation costs each side a lawyer fee.

**Backward induction:**

- On the **last** day (say the defendant makes the final offer), the plaintiff accepts any $s \ge J - C_P$ — what court is worth to him. The defendant offers exactly $J - C_P$, which beats paying $J + C_D$ in court.
- The day before, the plaintiff offers; the defendant accepts any $s$ up to what rejecting costs her: tomorrow's settlement **plus** one more day of fees. The plaintiff asks for exactly that.
- Working back, they **settle on day 1**, and the amount reflects who makes the **last** offer and each side's daily costs.

The court-cost saving $C_P + C_D$ is the surplus being divided; the side making the final offer captures most of it. This is why most lawsuits settle — and why procedural rules about the last offer matter.

## 10.3 Sequential bargaining

Two parties split a surplus of 1 (a joint-venture profit, a price range between a buyer's value and a seller's cost). Player 1 offers in odd rounds, Player 2 in even rounds; the responder accepts or rejects. A share received in round $t$ is worth $\delta_i^{t-1}$ to player $i$. If no agreement is reached by round $T$, both get 0.

**Backward induction:**

- In round $T$ the proposer offers 0 and keeps everything (the responder accepts 0 — rejecting also gives 0).
- In round $T - 1$ the proposer must give the responder what she would get as proposer in round $T$, **discounted**: $\delta_{\text{resp}} \times 1$.
- In general, the round-$t$ proposer keeps $1 - \delta_{\text{resp}} \times (\text{what the responder would keep as proposer in round } t+1)$.

Agreement is **immediate**; what matters is who has the **last word** and who is more **patient**.

## Key takeaways

- **Sophisticated voting**: early votes are votes for their eventual consequences; agenda control is real power.
- **Pre-trial bargaining** settles immediately; the litigation costs saved are split in favour of whoever has the final offer.
- **Alternating offers**: agreement is immediate; shares depend on the **last word** and on **patience** (higher δ = stronger bargainer).
