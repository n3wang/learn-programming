# 14. Bayesian Games: Static Games with Incomplete Information

> **Teaching rewrite** — follows the topic outline of MIT 14.12 lecture notes ch. 14 (MIT OpenCourseWare). Games and numbers are original.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. **Explain** incomplete information — players not knowing others' payoffs — and Harsanyi's trick of modelling it with a move by Nature.
2. **Define** a Bayesian game: players, types, a common prior, type-contingent strategies, and payoffs.
3. **Define** Bayesian Nash equilibrium (BNE) and compute it by checking each type's best response.
4. **Connect** BNE to the Nash equilibrium of the ex-ante normal form.

---

## 14.1 Bayesian games

So far every player knew everyone's payoffs. In business that is rare: a firm does not know a rival's costs, a bidder does not know others' valuations, a negotiator does not know how much the other side really wants the deal.

**Harsanyi's approach.** Summarise everything a player privately knows as her **type** $t_i$. Nature draws a type profile from a **common prior** $p$; each player observes her own type, then everyone moves.

A **Bayesian game** consists of:

- players $N = \{1, \dots, n\}$;
- for each player, a set of **types** $T_i$ and of **actions** $A_i$;
- a **common prior** $p$ over type profiles (players form beliefs about others' types by Bayes' rule given their own type);
- payoffs $u_i(a, t)$ depending on actions and types.

A **strategy** is a plan for **every type**: $s_i: T_i \to A_i$. You must specify what *each* type would do, because others' best responses depend on what they think every type of you does.

---

## 14.2 Bayesian Nash equilibrium

A profile $s^*$ is a **Bayesian Nash equilibrium** if, for every player $i$ and **every type** $t_i$, the action $s_i^*(t_i)$ maximises $i$'s **expected** payoff given her beliefs about others' types and the strategies $s_{-i}^*$:

$$
s_i^*(t_i) \in \arg\max_{a_i} \; \mathbb{E}\big[u_i(a_i, s_{-i}^*(t_{-i}), t) \mid t_i\big].
$$

Equivalently, $s^*$ is a Nash equilibrium of the **ex-ante** game in which strategies are type-contingent plans and payoffs are averaged over Nature's draw (when every type has positive probability). That is exactly what the tree tool computes when you reveal the normal form.

## 14.3 Example: does she want to meet you?

Player 1 wants to meet Player 2 at the Opera or the Football (Opera preferred). Player 2 is **Friendly** (wants to meet, prefers Football) or an **Avoider** (wants to avoid Player 1), each with probability ½. Player 2 knows her type; Player 1 does not. Both choose simultaneously.

<GameTreeLab preset="bayes" />

**Solve it type by type.** Guess that Player 1 plays Opera.

- **Friendly** Player 2: meeting at the Opera gives 1, going to Football alone gives 0 → **Opera**.
- **Avoider** Player 2: going to the Opera means meeting (0), Football means avoiding (2) → **Football**.
- **Player 1**, expecting (Opera if Friendly, Football if Avoider): Opera gives ½·2 + ½·0 = 1; Football gives ½·0 + ½·1 = ½ → **Opera**. ✓

So (Opera; Opera if Friendly, Football if Avoider) is a BNE. Check in the normal form that it is the only pure one.

## Key takeaways

- **Incomplete information** is modelled by **types** drawn by Nature from a **common prior**.
- A strategy is a **type-contingent plan**; you must say what every type does.
- **BNE**: every type of every player best-responds to the others' plans, using Bayes-consistent beliefs.
- BNE = Nash equilibrium of the ex-ante game (averaging over types).
