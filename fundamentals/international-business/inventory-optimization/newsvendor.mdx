# 10. Newsvendor Model

The **newsvendor** (or newsboy) model answers a single-period question: **how much should I produce or order before demand is known**, when you cannot replenish during the selling period and leftover units are costly?

Classic stories include daily newspapers, fashion seasons, event catering, and bakery items baked overnight. This lesson builds the model from a discrete muffin shop example, then generalizes overage and underage costs, expected profit, and the **critical ratio** for continuous and discrete demand.

Original teaching rewrite for this course; probability tables and numeric drills are constructed for practice, not copied from a commercial textbook.

## One-period bakery: chocolate muffins

Every evening you choose how many chocolate muffins to bake for tomorrow. Baking takes too long to react to same-day demand. From history you estimate **discrete** daily demand $D$ (integer units only):

| $d$ | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $p(d)$ | 0.40 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| $P(D\le x)$ | 0.40 | 0.60 | 0.80 | 0.90 | 0.95 | 1.00 |

Notation:

- $p(d)$ — **probability mass function (PMF)**: $\Pr(D=d)$.
- $P(D\le x)$ — **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** for discrete $D$ (same role as $F_D(x)$ for continuous demand).

Economics for one day:

- Unit cost $c=2$ (bake)
- Selling price $p=6$
- Unsold muffins are cleared at **salvage value** $s_v=1$ (discounted end-of-day price)

**Salvage value** is whatever you recover (or pay) when clearing leftover inventory—discounted sale, supplier buyback, or disposal cost.

### Profit for one demand outcome

Let $Q$ be the bake quantity and $d$ the realized demand. Sales are $\min(Q,d)$; leftovers are $\max(0,Q-d)$.

$$
\mathcal{P}(Q,d)=p\,\min(Q,d)+s_v\,\max(0,Q-d)-c\,Q
$$

Revenue from sales plus salvage on excess, minus full production cost for $Q$ units.

### Expected profit by $Q$

$$
\mathcal{P}(Q)=\sum_d \mathcal{P}(Q,d)\,p(d)
$$

For candidate quantities $Q\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$, expected profits are about **4, 6, 6, 5, 3.5** currency units. **$Q=4$ and $Q=6$** tie for the maximum (6); **$Q=4$** is the smallest maximizer among that grid. A full profit table by $(Q,d)$ is the same idea as a spreadsheet `SUMPRODUCT` over rows of demand.

## When is this a newsvendor problem?

Four structural conditions:

1. The order (or production run) is placed **before** the season or selling day.
2. **No replenishment** during the season.
3. **Overage cost** if you prepare too much.
4. **Underage cost** if you prepare too little (lost sales, penalties, or goodwill).

Examples: seasonal apparel, calendars, champagne for an event, and—at a higher level—safety stock over one replenishment cycle when the “season” is the time until the next order.

## Overage and underage unit costs

Split mismatches into two **unit** costs (positive numbers when they represent a cost):

| | Meaning | Typical muffin/cookie form |
|---|---|---|
| **Overage** $c_o$ | Cost of one unit left at period end | $c_o=c-s_v$ when unsold units are salvaged (not held to next period) |
| **Underage** $c_u$ | Cost of one lost sale (profit + penalties) | $c_u=p-c+g$; goodwill/penalty $g\ge 0$ optional |

If leftover units can be held to the next period at holding cost $h$, use $c_o=h$ instead of full write-off.

When the model **minimizes cost**, include lost **profit** in $c_u$. When it **maximizes profit** as in $\mathcal{P}(Q,d)$ above, do **not** double-count opportunity cost inside $c_u$.

For the muffins: $c_o=2-1=1$, $c_u=6-2=4$ (no goodwill).

## Expected sales, excess, and shortage

For each $(Q,d)$:

| Outcome | $(Q,d)$ | Discrete expectation in $Q$ |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | $\min(Q,d)$ | $\sum_{d\le Q} d\,p(d)$ |
| Excess units | $\max(0,Q-d)$ | $\sum_{d<Q}(Q-d)\,p(d)$ |
| Units short | $\max(0,d-Q)$ | $\sum_{d>Q}(d-Q)\,p(d)$ |

General profit decomposition (penalty/goodwill in $c_u$ only when using the cost form):

$$
\mathcal{P}(Q,d)=p\,\min(Q,d)-c_o\,\max(0,Q-d)-c_u\,\max(0,d-Q)-c\,Q
$$

Summing over $p(d)$ gives $\mathcal{P}(Q)$ in terms of $Q$ alone.

## Critical ratio and optimal order quantity

Define the **critical ratio** (target **cycle service level** at optimum):

$$
\alpha^*=\frac{c_u}{c_u+c_o}
$$

### Continuous demand

For continuous demand with CDF $F_D$, marginal balance on the $Q$-th unit gives:

$$
c_o F_D(Q)=c_u\bigl(1-F_D(Q)\bigr)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
F_D(Q^*)=\alpha^*
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
Q^*=F_D^{-1}(\alpha^*)
$$

**Cookie sketch (Normal):** $p=3$, $c=1$, no salvage → $c_u=2$, $c_o=1$, $\alpha^*=2/3\approx 66.7\%$. If demand is $\mathcal{N}(\mu=15,\sigma=5)$, then $Q^*\approx \mu+z_{0.67}\sigma\approx 17.2$—bake **17** if you round down to integer pans.

### Discrete demand

Increase $Q$ to $Q+1$ while expected underage savings beat overage:

$$
c_o P(D\le Q)\le c_u P(D>Q)
\;\Rightarrow\;
P(D\le Q)\le \alpha^*
$$

Stop at the **smallest** integer $Q$ with

$$
P(D\le Q)\ge \alpha^*
$$

For muffins, $\alpha^*=4/5=0.8$. On the lesson PMF, $P(D\le 4)=0.80$, so the critical-ratio rule selects **$Q^*=4$**—the same smallest maximizer as the expected-profit grid ($Q=4$ and $Q=6$ both earn 6 in expectation).

**Cookie sketch (discrete PMF, substitute product):** if each lost cookie still yields **1** in alternate margin, effective $c_u=p-c-1=1$, still $c_o=1$, $\alpha^*=50\%$. With a refined PMF on even counts, **$Q=14$** is the smallest quantity with $P(D\le 14)\ge 0.5$ (75% CDF), matching a full profit table.

## Cost-only form

Expected **cost** (with $c_u$ including lost profit per unit short):

$$
\mathcal{C}(Q)=c_o\sum_{d<Q}(Q-d)\,p(d)+c_u\sum_{d>Q}(d-Q)\,p(d)
$$

Minimizing $\mathcal{C}(Q)$ is equivalent to maximizing profit when definitions align. At the optimum, total expected overage and underage costs balance—a useful Excel sanity check.

## Takeaways

- Newsvendor fits **one-period**, **no-replenishment** decisions with both overage and underage mismatch costs.
- Discrete demand uses a **PMF** and CDF; continuous demand uses a density and $F_D^{-1}$.
- **Critical ratio** $\alpha^*=c_u/(c_u+c_o)$ sets optimal service: $Q^*=F_D^{-1}(\alpha^*)$ (continuous) or smallest $Q$ with $P(D\le Q)\ge\alpha^*$ (discrete).
- Expected profit $\mathcal{P}(Q)=\sum_d \mathcal{P}(Q,d)p(d)$ is a universal check when the PMF is custom or messy.
- Mismatch costs can include salvage revenue, holding, goodwill, or penalties—define $c_o$ and $c_u$ consistently with whether you maximize profit or minimize cost.
