# 6. Inventory Policy Simulation Lab

The earlier chapters derived policy settings under clear assumptions. In this lab, turn those settings into executable Python and inspect a small, reproducible demand trace. The data is deliberately supplied rather than randomly sampled: that makes timing and expected outputs easy to verify before you experiment with random demand.

The exercises use Python's standard library only. They focus on three practical tasks:

1. Calculate continuous and periodic safety-stock targets.
2. Simulate an $(R,S)$ policy with a fixed lead time and lost sales.
3. Classify current on-hand against policy inventory reference levels.

## Start with the protection-period targets

Given per-period mean demand $\mu$, standard deviation $\sigma$, lead time $L$, review interval $R$, and service factor $z$, calculate:

$$
SS_{s,Q}=z\sigma\sqrt{L},\qquad s=\mu L+SS_{s,Q}
$$

$$
SS_{R,S}=z\sigma\sqrt{L+R},\qquad S=\mu(L+R)+SS_{R,S}
$$

Use the demand and time units consistently. The test data supplies $z$ directly, so the exercise can focus on translating the policy equations into code.

| Case | Mean demand / period | Demand SD / period | $L$ | $R$ | $z$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 40 | 13.6 | 4 | 1 | 1.645 |
| B | 100 | 10 | 4 | 5 | 1.0 |

## Simulate a periodic-review policy

Use this six-period demand trace to test the order/receipt timing. The policy reviews every $R=2$ periods, targets $S=8$ units of inventory position, and has lead time $L=1$ period. Start with 8 units on hand and no pipeline stock. Unfilled demand is **lost**, not backordered.

| Period | Demand, trace A | Demand, trace B |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 2 | 7 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | 6 | 2 |
| 5 | 1 | 2 |
| 6 | 4 | 2 |

Use these timing rules:

- Receive any shipment due at the start of a period, then satisfy as much demand as on-hand stock allows.
- At the end of every second period, order $\max(0,S-\text{inventory position})$.
- Inventory position is on-hand plus outstanding pipeline orders; lost sales are not subtracted as backorders.
- An order placed at the end of period $t$ with $L=1$ becomes available at the start of period $t+2$, after one full intervening period in transit.
- A cycle is a block of $R$ periods. A cycle is successful only if every period in it has no stockout; period service counts individual no-stockout periods.

## Key takeaways

- Check inventory position and expected stock components separately; pipeline is not on-hand stock.
- Keep each policy's protection period and order rule explicit.
- Simulation results depend on receipt timing, review timing, and lost-sales versus backorder treatment.
- Data tables make a run inspectable; multiple deterministic tests catch timing and boundary errors before random demand is introduced.
