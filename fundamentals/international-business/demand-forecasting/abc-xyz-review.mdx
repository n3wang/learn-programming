# 13. What Do You Review? ABC XYZ Segmentation and Other Methods

> **Teaching rewrite** — Part 3, the *Review process* step: where should limited planner time go? Original wording; illustrative numbers.

## Introduction

A model can forecast every item; planners can’t review every item. Each cycle you must choose a small set — the products that are **important** to the supply chain **and** where a human is **likely to improve** the baseline. Value-weighted KPIs (lesson 11) say which items matter; FVA (lesson 12) says whether reviews help. Neither says **which items to open first**. That’s this lesson.

This lesson covers:

- **ABC** analysis and **ABC XYZ** matrices
- Setting them up correctly: **forward-looking value** and **historical forecast error**
- Their **limits**
- A **multi-criteria** star rating that goes beyond two dimensions

---

## 13.1 ABC analysis

ABC (Pareto, 80/20) splits items into three classes along **one** dimension:

| Approach | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| By share of items | Top **5%** of items (~20–60% of volume) | Next **15%** (~20–50%) | Bottom **80%** (~5–20%) |
| By share of volume | Items making the first **60%** of volume (often 5–15% of items) | Next **20%** (15–30% of items) | Last **20%** (50–80% of items) |
| Priority | High — the *critical few* | Medium | Low — the *trivial many* |

### ABC XYZ

Add a second dimension (**XYZ**) → a 3 × 3 matrix with nine classes, e.g. **volume** (ABC) × **profit** (XYZ), from high-volume/high-profit (AZ) to low-volume/low-profit (CX).

---

## 13.2 Setting ABC XYZ up for forecast review

Our goal: point planners to items that are **important** and **hard for the model**. So:

| Axis | Measures | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ABC — importance** | **Forward-looking, value-weighted** | Next X months’ **forecast × unit value** | Historical sales (backward-looking; constrained by shortages; misses seasonality, promotions, trends) |
| **XYZ — forecastability** | How badly the engine did on its own | **Historical forecast error**, e.g. Score% = MAE% + \|Bias%\| | **COV** of demand (inflated by trend and seasonality; leaks hindsight — lesson 10) |

| Forecastability ↓ / Importance → | **A** (high value) | **B** | **C** (low value) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Z** (high error) | **AZ — review first** | BZ | CZ — review if time |
| **Y** | AY | BY | CY |
| **X** (low error) | AX — spot-check | BX | **CX — leave to the engine** |

Review **A-Z** first (important, poorly forecast); leave **C-X** (unimportant, well forecast) to the engine.

### Worked example: classify five items

Forecast value next 6 months and historical Score%:

| Item | Forecast value (6 mo) | Score% | ABC | XYZ | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | €400k | 55% | A | Z | **AZ — review first** |
| P2 | €350k | 12% | A | X | AX — spot-check |
| P3 | €60k | 48% | B | Z | BZ |
| P4 | €8k | 70% | C | Z | CZ |
| P5 | €5k | 10% | C | X | CX — leave to engine |

(Thresholds here: A ≥ €200k, C < €20k; X < 20%, Z ≥ 40%.)

## 13.3 Limits of ABC XYZ

ABC XYZ was state of the art half a century ago, when planners had no computers to analyse thousands of items. Its limits:

- **Only two dimensions.** Lead time, lead-time reliability, shelf life, obsolescence risk, business criticality, and promotions don’t fit.
- **Arbitrary thresholds.** Is the best B really different from the worst A? Two cut-offs per axis is coarse.

Use it for simple analyses — but don’t trust it blindly.

---

## 13.4 Beyond ABC XYZ: multi-criteria review

Today you can score every item on many criteria automatically. Review items where:

- You have **information the model lacks** — promotions, marketing, customer actions
- It is a **new product** introduction
- The forecast behaves **strangely** — persistent over/under-forecasting, sudden shifts
- **Errors are expensive** — high cost, long lead time, special storage (frozen), short shelf life (food, medicine)
- **End of season** is near — risk of dead stock
- It is **strategically critical** — service-level agreements, spare parts needed to sell equipment
- It is **sold together** with other items — nuts and bolts should move together

### A star rating

| Criterion | Stars |
|---|---|
| Good historical forecastability | −1 |
| Small volume/value | −1 |
| End of season | +1 |
| Promotion planned | +2 |
| Brand-new product | +3 |
| Critical product | +3 |

Start every item at a base (say 2 stars), apply the rules, review from the highest.

### Worked example: star scores

| Item | Situation | Calculation | Stars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | New product with a launch promotion | 2 + 3 + 2 | **7** |
| Q2 | Critical spare part, forecast well | 2 + 3 − 1 | **4** |
| Q3 | Seasonal item, end of season, small value | 2 + 1 − 1 | **2** |
| Q4 | Small, stable item | 2 − 1 − 1 | **0** |

Review order: Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 (likely skipped).

:::caution Critical ≠ inflate
A critical product deserves **attention**, not a **higher forecast** “to be safe”. Protection against shortages is the inventory manager’s job (service levels, safety stock). Inflated forecasts erode trust and lead to double-guessing — “planners always inflate, so I’ll cut my stock target”.
:::

## Takeaways

:::tip Remember
- Review items that are **important** and where you are **likely to add value**
- ABC = **forward-looking value-weighted forecast**; XYZ = **historical forecast error** — not historical sales, not COV
- ABC XYZ is simple but limited: **two dimensions, arbitrary thresholds**
- Go **multi-criteria**: promotions, launches, odd behaviour, cost, shelf life, end of season, criticality, related items
- **Critical** means review carefully — not inflate the forecast
:::
