# 9. Choosing the Best Forecasting KPI

> **Teaching rewrite** — the *Metrics* step. Original wording; examples recomputed.

## Introduction

Lesson 8 showed four metrics crowning four different forecasts. This lesson stress-tests them on two hard cases — **outliers** and **intermittent demand** — and lands on a practical recommendation: track **MAE and bias together**, optionally combined into one **score**.

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Bias | average(error) |
| MAE | average(\|error\|) |
| MAE% | sum(\|error\|) / sum(demand) |
| MAPE | average(\|error\| / demand) |
| RMSE | √average(error²) |

---

## 9.1 Extreme demand (outliers)

| Metric | Reaction to one huge demand spike |
|---|---|
| **RMSE** | **Over-reacts** — the squared error of the spike can dominate the whole score |
| **MAPE** | **Barely reacts** — the spike’s error is divided by the huge demand, and a too-low forecast’s error is capped at 100% |
| **MAE** | Reacts **proportionately** — affected, but not dominated |

### Worked example: one outlier

Demand 100, 100, 100, **400**; forecast 100 every period → errors 0, 0, 0, −300.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| MAE | 300 / 4 = 75 → MAE% = 300 / 700 = **42.9%** |
| RMSE | √(90,000 / 4) = **150** → RMSE% = 150 / 175 = **85.7%** |
| MAPE | (0 + 0 + 0 + 75%) / 4 = **18.75%** |

One spike doubles RMSE% relative to MAE%, while MAPE makes the miss look small.

---

## 9.2 Intermittent demand

Many items sell only occasionally. Example: demand over 12 periods is mostly zero, with sales of 9, 6, 12, 9 every third period (average **3** per period).

| Forecast | Bias% | MAE% | RMSE% | MAPE | Score (MAE% + \|Bias%\|) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A**: 3 every period | **0%** | 133.3% | **147.2%** | undefined | **133.3%** |
| **B**: 0 every period | −100% | **100%** | 178.0% | undefined | 200% |

- **MAE prefers B** — forecasting **zero** forever — because the **median** of intermittent demand is 0. A forecast of zero is useless to a planner.
- **MAPE** can’t be computed at all (division by zero).
- **RMSE** and **bias** prefer A, the useful forecast.
- The **combined score** also prefers A.

## 9.3 The best compromise: MAE + bias

| Metric | Simple | Unbiased | Error weighting | Outlier sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MAE** | ✅ | ❌ (aims at median) | Uniform | Moderate ✅ |
| **MAPE** | ✅ | ❌ (pushes down) | Over-weights low-demand periods 💥 | Too low ❌ |
| **RMSE** | ❌ | ✅ | Over-weights big errors | Too high ❌ |
| **MAE + Bias** | ✅ | ✅ | Uniform | Moderate ✅ |

No single metric is perfect, so **combine two**:

> **Score = MAE + |Bias|**  **Score% = MAE% + |Bias%|**

The bias term pulls MAE’s median-seeking (downward) tendency back toward the mean.

:::caution Use the absolute bias
Forget the absolute value and you **reward under-forecasting** (negative bias would lower the score).
:::

- Use the **score** to compare forecasts quantitatively.
- With management, show **MAE and bias separately** — easier to understand than a combined percentage.
- Track it **for every relevant lag** (lesson 6); analysts look per lag, management may prefer a single scorecard.

## 9.4 Case study — part 2

| Forecast | Bias% | MAE% | MAPE | RMSE% | **Score%** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** (380) | −0.5% | 68.4% | 352.9% | 94.4% | **68.9%** |
| **2** (260) | −31.9% | 65.7% | 239.1% | 99.6% | 97.6% |
| **3** (200) | −47.6% | 67.6% | 187.1% | 105.7% | 115.2% |

Forecast 1 wins clearly: 68.4 + 0.5 = **68.9%**.

Forecasts 2 and 3 carry huge negative bias (−32% and −48%). Any consistent bias beyond about **±5%** should be treated as suspicious — something is wrong with the model or the process. If safety stocks are set in **days of cover**, under-forecasting also shrinks the buffers, compounding shortages.

How to say it to management: *“Forecast 1 is the best balance of accuracy and bias, and both matter for running the supply chain.”*

## Takeaways

:::tip Remember
- **RMSE** over-reacts to outliers; **MAPE** under-reacts and breaks on zeros; **MAE** alone can favour a zero forecast for intermittent items
- Use **MAE + bias** — or **Score = MAE + |Bias|** — as the default
- Treat consistent **|bias| > ~5%** as a red flag
- Show MAE and bias **separately** to management; compute per **lag**
- **Avoid MAPE**
:::
