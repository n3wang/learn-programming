## Expectation, variance, and standard deviation

For a random variable $X$ with density/mass $f$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \int x\, f(x)\, dx
\quad\text{(or }\sum x f(x)\text{ if discrete)}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2 \ge 0
$$

Standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)}$. Conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y=y]$ uses the conditional density. Covariance and correlation:

$$
\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb{E}[XY]-\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y], \qquad
\rho = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} \in [-1,1]
$$

## Uniform moments (interview derivation target)

If $X \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[a,b]$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X]=\frac{a+b}{2}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(X)=\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}
$$

You should be able to re-derive these from the PDF $1/(b-a)$ when asked.

## Law of Large Numbers

Independently sample $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ from the same distribution. The sample mean $\bar X_n$ converges to $\mathbb{E}[X]$ as $n\to\infty$. Short streaks (five heads) do not contradict LLN — long-run averages do.

## Central Limit Theorem

For large $n$, $\bar X_n$ is approximately Normal with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2/n$, even if $X$ itself is not Normal:

$$
\frac{\bar X_n - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \approx N(0,1)
$$

CLT underwrites most z-tests and A/B proportion tests.

## Hypothesis testing

1. State $H_0$ (baseline) and $H_1$ (what you hope to support).  
2. Compute a test statistic under $H_0$ → **p-value**.  
3. Compare to significance level $\alpha$. Reject $H_0$ if $p < \alpha$. Failing to reject is not proof $H_0$ is true.

**One-sided:** $H_0:\mu=\mu_0$ vs $H_1:\mu>\mu_0$ (or $<$).  
**Two-sided:** $H_1:\mu \ne \mu_0$.

A/B testing is hypothesis testing on product metrics (e.g. equal conversion rates vs uplift).

### Test statistics

| Test | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Z** | Large $n$ (CLT) or known $\sigma$ |
| **t** | Small $n$, $\sigma$ unknown (use $s$, df $=n-1$) |
| **χ²** | Goodness of fit / independence in contingency tables |

$$
z = \frac{\bar x - \mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}, \qquad
t = \frac{\bar x - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
$$

### Proportions and A/B

Members are Bernoulli; $\hat p$ is a mean. Under $H_0:p=p_0$:

$$
z = \frac{\hat p - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}
$$

Two-sample A/B uses a pooled SE for $\hat p_B - \hat p_A$. Always name the statistic **and** that CLT justifies Normality for large $n$.

## p-values and confidence intervals

A **p-value** is the probability, under $H_0$, of a test statistic at least as extreme as observed. Small $p$ → data are surprising under $H_0$.

A $(1-\alpha)$ **confidence interval** is a random interval that covers the true parameter in $(1-\alpha)$ of repeated samples. For a mean with known $\sigma$:

$$
\bar x \pm z_{\alpha/2}\cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

For a proportion: $\hat p \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$. If the null value (e.g. 0 lift) lies inside the CI, you typically do not reject $H_0$.

## Type I / II errors, power, Bonferroni

| | Reject $H_0$ | Fail to reject |
|---|---|---|
| $H_0$ true | **Type I** (false positive), rate $\alpha$ | Correct |
| $H_0$ false | Correct (power $1-\beta$) | **Type II** (false negative), rate $\beta$ |

Larger $n$ generally raises power. For $m$ tests at family-wise $\alpha$, **Bonferroni** uses per-test $\alpha/m$ so you do not expect $m\alpha$ false positives by chance.

## MLE and MAP

**MLE:** $\hat\theta = \arg\max_\theta L(\theta)$ with $L(\theta)=\prod_i f(x_i\mid\theta)$. Work with $\log L = \sum \log f(x_i\mid\theta)$.

**MAP:** maximize prior × likelihood: $\arg\max_\theta g(\theta)\, f(\mathbf{x}\mid\theta)$.

Classic drill: Bernoulli MLE is $\hat p = k/n$; Normal mean MLE is the sample mean.

## Interview checklist

- Moments: $\mathbb{E}$, Var, $\sigma$, Cov, $\rho$  
- Uniform (and friends): re-derive $\mathbb{E}$ / Var when asked  
- LLN → long-run averages; CLT → $\bar X$ ≈ Normal, SE $=\sigma/\sqrt{n}$  
- Hypothesis test steps; Z vs t vs χ²; A/B = proportion z + CLT  
- Explain p-value and CI in plain language  
- Type I/II, power, Bonferroni for many metrics  
- MLE (log-likelihood) vs MAP (prior)
