> **Source:** Concepts adapted from *Ace the Data Science Interview* — Chapter 10 (“Product Sense”) by Kevin Huo & Nick Singh. Rewritten in original wording for this course.

Product-focused DS interviews test whether you can partner with PMs: pick metrics, dig into weird changes, brainstorm features, and design honest experiments. Technical chops still matter — but here the bar is judgment, structure, and company context. Pretend you already work there and you’re in a working meeting with a teammate.

## Four common question types

| Type | Example prompt |
| --- | --- |
| **Define a metric** | How would you measure success of a new launch / feature change? |
| **Diagnose a metric change** | Engagement dropped — how do you find the root cause? What about trade-offs with counter-metrics? |
| **Brainstorm product / features** | Should we launch X? What features would move metric Y? |
| **Design an A/B test** | How would you test feature Z? What pitfalls, and how do you handle them? |

Keep these templates in mind as you read the frameworks below.

## How to approach any product question

- **Clarify** — user flow, end users, stakeholders, product & business goals. Even if you researched the company, phrase knowledge as a check-in (“Robinhood’s mission is… this crypto wallet seems aimed at… am I on track?”).
- **Scope** — these prompts are huge; say what you’re deliberately ignoring for the interview clock.
- **Talk out loud** — they hired you to hear your thinking.
- **Be conversational** — check in (“I’ll use avg watch time as the engagement north star — sound good?”).
- **Keep goals forefront** — answers hang off mission / vision you stated early.
- **Bring experience tactfully** — real-world parallels help; “this is the only way because Google did it” does not.

Ultimate cheat code: behave like you’re already on the team.

## Building product & business sense

**Product sense** is a skill, not a birthright. Daily habit — when you use a product, ask:

- Who was it created for?
- What main problem does it solve?
- What end-user *benefits* go beyond the narrow problem?
- How do design / copy reinforce purpose?
- How does it tie to company mission?

Empathy matters. Snapchat vs Instagram look similar at a glance but optimize different jobs (ephemeral authentic sharing vs polished feed consumption). Calibrate with **reviews** (App Store, Play, Reddit, G2 / Gartner, Amazon) — positives articulate benefits; negatives spark brainstorm ideas.

**Business sense** (even if “model free cash flow” is rare): follow the money so you work on leveraged problems.

- Monetization model & levers
- KPIs and what moves them
- Landscape — competitors & partners

Homework that levels the playing field against bubble-dwelling interviewers / internal candidates:

1. Business model + revenue mix (earnings / analyst coverage / comps if private)
2. Marketplace or SaaS vocabulary for that company type
3. Engineering blog posts (Uber Eats delivery ETA, food discovery, etc.)
4. **Use the product from every side** (eater + driver + restaurant portal for Uber Eats)

Yes, it’s a lot — and “Did you try our product? Ideas?” is a real question. Research also upgrades your closing questions.

## Metrics: AARRR + engagement

Dave McClure’s **pirate metrics** (AARRR):

| Stage | Question | Example metrics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Acquisition** | How do users find you? | New users, signup conversion, CAC |
| **Activation** | Great first experience / “aha”? | First order, first post, 10 feed views |
| **Retention** | Do they come back? | Monthly retention / churn |
| **Referral** | Do they tell others? | Invites × invite conversion = **k-factor** (k>1 → viral) |
| **Revenue** | How do you make money? | LTV, paid upgrades, ads; aim for healthy **LTV/CAC** |

**Engagement** (often called out even if not a letter in AARRR): intensity / frequency of core actions — time spent, rides booked, **DAU / WAU / MAU**, stickiness ≈ DAU/MAU.

Do homework for the company type (SaaS → ACV, MRR; marketplaces → GMV, take rate). *Lean Analytics* is a solid deeper dive.

## What makes a metric good or bad?

Bad metrics (Facebook Dating examples):

| Flaw | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| **Vanity** | Profiles viewed — sounds busy, weak signal of meaningful relationships |
| **Irrelevant** | Time spent — great for YouTube, weak for activity-driven dating value |
| **Impractical** | # of 3rd dates — hard/impossible to measure reliably |
| **Complicated** | Stakeholders can’t explain a move without dissecting the formula |
| **Delayed** | # of marriages — too laggy for product decisions |

Good metrics reverse those: **meaningful** (tied to goals, hard to game, actionable), **measurable**, **understandable**, **timely** (leading where possible).

Always pair a **north star** with **guardrail / counter metrics** you refuse to trash while optimizing (e.g. cut harmful content *without* nuking posts / views / comments).

## Framework: define success metrics (3 steps)

Example: “How would you measure success of Facebook Dating?”

1. **Clarify product & purpose** — business why, casual vs serious dating, audience, standalone vs in-app, UI mechanics, launch market.
2. **State product & business goals** — synthesize (don’t only ask); tie to mission (meaningful relationships) and ecosystem (engagement → ads, keep people in Meta apps).
3. **Define metrics along the funnel** — acquisition (signups / profiles), activation (rich profile, first match), engagement (matches, swipes, meet signals), retention, revenue (subs / ads) — and say what matters *at launch* (often PMF / retention before monetization).

## Framework: diagnose a metric change (4 steps)

Example: “Avg comments per Instagram post is declining — troubleshoot.”

1. **Scope** — definition (ratio? which part moved?), importance, timeframe, magnitude (WoW vs YoY), absolute vs relative.
2. **Hypothesize** in buckets: **accidental** (logging bugs), **natural** (seasonality), **internal** (UI / ranking / features), **external** (competitors, macro). Walk **up the funnel** from local feature → broader surfaces; don’t dump 20 theories — prioritize.
3. **Validate** — segment (demo, device, cohort), compare likes/shares vs comments, feed vs Stories/Reels, comment composer views.
4. **Classify** findings: root cause / contributing / correlated symptom / unrelated.

Classic ending: onboarding interstitial teaching “turn off comments” created more comment-disabled posts → average comments/post fell even while commenting health on comment-enabled posts was fine.

### Metric trade-offs

“Ads +1% revenue, time spent −3% — ship?” Clarify both metrics, map to strategy, then recommend **revert**, **mitigate** (hide ad / better targeting), or **accept** — often conditional on how hard each % is to earn back. Interviewers want judgment process, not a single “correct” ship bit.

## A/B testing in product interviews

After metrics definition you’ll often hear “How would you test it?” Cover four setup steps:

1. **Pick primary metric(s)** (+ guardrails) — no post-hoc cherry-picking  
2. **Thresholds** — α≈0.05, power≈0.8, MDE with stakeholders  
3. **Sample size & length** — from variance / MDE / traffic; often ≥2 weeks for day-of-week  
4. **Random assignment** — avoid confounding (platform may handle this)

### When *not* to A/B test

No infrastructure, tiny impact vs cost, no traffic, no conviction (random monkey variants), no isolation (logo change hits headlines). Alternatives: UX research, log analysis, ship + retrospective.

### Practical pitfalls

| Pitfall | Idea |
| --- | --- |
| Non-normal metrics | Bootstrap, rank tests, more data |
| Multiple testing | Bonferroni / FDR / FWER; dig into overlapping experiments |
| Network effects | Spillover understates effects; cluster / graph partition randomization |
| Novelty / primacy | Spike or backlash then settle; new-user slices / longer runs / holdouts |
| Significant ≠ ship | Effect size, guardrails, eng/support cost, bug risk |
| Holdouts | Tiny long-run control after launch — skip for sensitive safety fixes |

## Quick synthesis

| Topic | Sound bite |
| --- | --- |
| Four prompts | Metrics, diagnosis, brainstorm, A/B |
| Approach | Clarify, scope, converse, mission-first |
| Prep | Use the product, read blogs/earnings, learn model-type KPIs |
| Funnel | AARRR + engagement; k-factor; LTV/CAC |
| Metric quality | Meaningful, measurable, clear, timely + guardrails |
| Diagnosis | Scope → hypothesize → validate → classify |
| Experiments | Design upfront; know when not to test; watch networks & novelty |
