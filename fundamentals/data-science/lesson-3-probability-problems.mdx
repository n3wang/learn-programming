  <Problem
    title="5.1"
    company="Google"
    prompt={`Two teams play a best-of-7 series (first to 4 wins). Each game is an independent coin flip — each team has probability 1/2 of winning any game (no draws). What is the probability the series goes the full 7 games?`}
    answer={0.31}
    why={`The series reaches game 7 iff each team has exactly 3 wins after 6 games: C(6,3)·(1/2)^6 = 20/64 = 0.3125 → 0.31.`}

    answer={0.05}

  <Problem
    title="5.3"
    company="Uber"
    prompt={`You roll three fair dice one after another. What is the probability that the three numbers are strictly increasing?`}
    answer={0.09}
    why={`Choose 3 distinct faces C(6,3)=20; only one increasing order each. 20/216 = 5/54 ≈ 0.0926 → 0.09.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.4"
    company="Zenefits"
    prompt={`A deck has 100 cards labeled 1 through 100. You draw two cards at random without replacement. What is the probability that one number is exactly double the other?`}
    answer={0.01}
    why={`Favorable pairs (n,2n) for n=1..50 → 50 pairs. Total C(100,2)=4950. 50/4950 = 1/99 ≈ 0.0101 → 0.01.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.5"
    company="JP Morgan"
    prompt={`In 3D space, how many shortest paths are there from (0,0,0) to (3,3,3) if each step increases exactly one coordinate by 1 (only “up”, “right”, or “forward”)? Enter the integer count.`}
    answer={1680}
    decimals={0}
    why={`You need 9 steps: 3 of each type. Number of sequences = 9!/(3!3!3!) = 1680.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.6"
    company="Amazon"
    prompt={`One in a thousand people have a disease. A test is correct 98% of the time when the person has the disease, and has a 1% false-positive rate when they do not. Someone tests positive. What is the probability they have the disease?`}
    answer={0.09}
    why={`Bayes: 0.98·0.001 / (0.98·0.001 + 0.01·0.999) ≈ 0.00098/0.01097 ≈ 0.0893 → 0.09.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.7"
    company="Facebook"
    prompt={`There are two coins: one fair (H/T) and one double-tailed. You pick a coin at random, flip it five times, and see TTTTT. What is the probability you are flipping the unfair coin?`}
    answer={0.97}
    why={`Bayes: P(unfair|data) ∝ 1·(1/2) vs (1/32)·(1/2) → 32/33 ≈ 0.9697 → 0.97.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.8"
    company="Goldman Sachs"
    prompt={`Players A and B take turns flipping a coin with P(heads)=p; first heads wins. A starts. For the fair case p = 0.5, what is the probability that A wins?`}
    answer={0.67}
    why={`In general P(A)=p/(1−(1−p)^2)=1/(2−p). For p=1/2: 1/1.5 = 2/3 ≈ 0.666… → 0.67.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.9"
    company="Microsoft"
    prompt={`Prior probability of rain in Seattle on a given day is 0.25. Three friends each independently say it is rainy; each lies with probability 1/3. What is the probability it is actually rainy given all three reports?`}
    answer={0.73}
    why={`P(3 say rain | rain)=(2/3)^3, P(3 say rain | dry)=(1/3)^3. Posterior = 0.25·8/27 / (0.25·8/27 + 0.75·1/27) = 8/11 ≈ 0.727 → 0.73.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.10"
    company="Bloomberg"
    prompt={`You draw a circle and choose two chords uniformly at random (via four random endpoints on the circumference). What is the probability that the two chords intersect inside the circle?`}
    answer={0.33}
    why={`Four random points on a circle: chords join them in crossing pairs in 1 of 3 equally likely pairings → 1/3 ≈ 0.33.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.11"
    company="Morgan Stanley"
    prompt={`You and a friend toss a fair coin until HH or TH appears. You win on HH; your friend wins on TH. What is the probability that you win?`}
    answer={0.25}
    why={`Markov states on the prefix: P(win from start)=1/4. Intuition: the first H is almost surely preceded by T or nothing in a way that favors TH; solving the chain gives 0.25.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.12"
    company="JP Morgan"
    prompt={`You may roll a fair 6-sided die up to twice and can stop after the first roll. Your payoff is the face value of the final roll (in dollars). Under optimal play, what is the expected value of the game (how much are you willing to pay)?`}
    answer={4.25}
    why={`Second-roll EV is 3.5, so reroll on 1–3 and keep 4–6. Value = (3·3.5 + 4 + 5 + 6)/6 = 25.5/6 = 4.25.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.13"
    company="Facebook"
    prompt={`90% of raters are diligent: they mark content spam with probability 0.20 (so “good” with 0.80). The other 10% never mark spam (always “good”). Labels are independent. A rater marks four pieces as good. What is the probability this rater is diligent?`}
    answer={0.79}
    why={`Bayes: 0.9·0.8^4 / (0.9·0.8^4 + 0.1·1) = 0.36864/0.46864 ≈ 0.7866 → 0.79.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.14"
    company="D.E. Shaw"
    prompt={`A couple has two children. You learn that at least one is a boy. Assuming each child is independently boy/girl with equal probability, what is the probability both are boys?`}
    answer={0.33}
    why={`Equally likely BB, BG, GB, GG; condition on not GG leaves three cases, one of which is BB → 1/3 ≈ 0.33.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.15"
    company="JP Morgan"
    prompt={`A desk has 8 drawers. With probability 1/2 a letter was placed uniformly at random in one drawer; with probability 1/2 there is no letter at all. You open the first 7 drawers and all are empty. What is the probability the 8th drawer contains the letter?`}
    answer={0.11}
    why={`P(L_8 ∩ empty first 7)=1/16; P(empty first 7)=1/2 + 1/16 = 9/16. Posterior = (1/16)/(9/16) = 1/9 ≈ 0.111 → 0.11.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.16"
    company="Optiver"
    prompt={`Tennis deuce: play until one player leads by two points. Player 1 wins each point with probability 0.60 (independently). What is the probability player 1 wins the game from deuce?`}
    answer={0.69}
    why={`From deuce, P = p^2 + 2p(1−p)P ⇒ P = p^2/(1−2p(1−p)) = 0.36/0.52 ≈ 0.6923 → 0.69.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.17"
    company="Facebook"
    prompt={`A deck has 50 cards: 5 colors × numbers 1–10 (one card per color-number pair). You draw two cards at random. What is the probability they have different colors and different numbers?`}
    answer={0.73}
    why={`Total C(50,2)=1225. Same color: 5·C(10,2)=225. Same number: 10·C(5,2)=100. Neither: 1 − 225/1225 − 100/1225 = 900/1225 = 36/49 ≈ 0.7347 → 0.73.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.18"
    company="SIG"
    prompt={`You roll ten fair six-sided dice at once. What is the probability that the sum of the top faces is divisible by 6?`}
    answer={0.17}
    why={`A fair die is uniform on residues mod 6, so the sum of any number of independent fair dice is also uniform mod 6 → exactly 1/6 ≈ 0.1667 → 0.17.`}
  />
