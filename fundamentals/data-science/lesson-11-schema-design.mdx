> **Original interactive lab for this course** — built on the Reddit-style `users` / `posts` schema from the [SQL lesson](./lesson-10-sql), extended here with a `comments` table for the exercise below.

Query-writing is only half of the "SQL & DB design" interview question — the other half is reading (or drawing) an entity-relationship diagram and reasoning about foreign keys. This lab is a small draggable canvas: cards represent tables, and you connect them the way you'd sketch a schema on a whiteboard.

## Explore: the shipped schema

This is the exact two-table schema the SQL lesson queries against. Drag the cards around, or rewire the connection — nothing here is graded, it's just the same diagram rendered as a canvas instead of a markdown table.

## Exercise: wire the extended schema, and spot the missing column

Reddit posts also have comments. Below are three tables — `users`, `posts`, and a new `comments` table — with **one bug**: `comments` is missing the foreign key back to `users` (right now it only knows which post it's under, not who wrote it). You'll need to:

1. **Add the missing field.** Click the dashed `+ field name` row inside `comments`, type `user_id`, give it the badge `FK`, and press Enter (or click **+**).
2. **Wire the foreign keys**, field to field: drag from `posts.user_id` to `users.user_id`, from `comments.post_id` to `posts.post_id`, and from the `comments.user_id` you just added to `users.user_id`. Direction matters (drag from the table that *has* the FK to the table it references); which side of the card you start from doesn't.

Click **Check** once you think it's done — after two wrong checks, whatever's still missing is revealed.

`comments` ends up with **two** foreign keys — one to `posts`, one to `users` — which is exactly the kind of "wait, doesn't this table need another FK" detail interviewers probe for when they hand you a schema with a subtly missing column.
