  <Problem
    title="5.29"
    company="D.E. Shaw"
    prompt={`You have 150 friends. Each friend's last-four phone digits are equally likely to be any of the $10^4$ combinations, independently. What is the probability that exactly 3 of your 150 friends have last-four-digits that are some permutation of 0, 1, 4, and 9?`}
    answer={0.0054}
    decimals={4}
    why={`A given friend's number matches with probability $p = 4!/10^4 = 24/10000 = 0.0024$ (any of the $4!$ orderings of those four digits). With 150 independent friends, $P(\\text{exactly }3) = \\binom{150}{3}p^3(1-p)^{147} \\approx 0.00535 \\to 0.0054$.`}

  <Problem
    title="5.30"
    company="Spotify"
    prompt={`A fair six-sided die is rolled $n=5$ times. What is the probability that the largest value rolled is exactly $r=4$?`}
    answer={0.1}
    why={`Let $B_r$ be "all $n$ rolls are $\\le r$": $P(B_r) = (r/6)^n$. The largest roll equals $r$ exactly when $B_r$ holds but $B_{r-1}$ doesn't: $P = (r^n - (r-1)^n)/6^n$. For $r=4, n=5$: $(4^5-3^5)/6^5 = (1024-243)/7776 = 781/7776 \\approx 0.1004 \\to 0.10$. The same formula gives the distribution for any $r \\in \\{1,\\dots,6\\}$.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.31"
    company="Goldman Sachs"
    prompt={`A jar starts with a single amoeba. Every minute, each living amoeba independently has a 1-in-4 chance of each of: dying, doing nothing, splitting into 2, or splitting into 3. What is the probability the jar eventually has no living amoeba?`}
    answer={0.41}
    why={`Let $p$ be the extinction probability. It satisfies the fixed point $p = \\frac14(1+p+p^2+p^3)$ — die / do nothing / split-2 / split-3, each needing all resulting amoeba lines to also go extinct. This factors as $(p-1)(p^2+2p-1)=0$; the relevant root in $[0,1)$ is $p = \\sqrt2-1 \\approx 0.4142 \\to 0.41$.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.32"
    company="Lyft"
    prompt={`A fair coin is tossed $n=10$ times, landing on $k=4$ heads. What is the probability that the first toss was heads?`}
    answer={0.4}
    why={`Given $k$ heads total, every arrangement of the $n$ tosses with that many heads is equally likely, so by symmetry each of the $n$ positions is equally likely to hold one of the $k$ heads. $P(\\text{first toss heads}) = k/n = 4/10 = 0.4$.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.33"
    company="Quora"
    prompt={`You take $N=5$ i.i.d. draws from a Normal($\\mu=0, \\sigma=1$) distribution. What is the probability that exactly $k=2$ of the draws are larger than $Y=1$?`}
    answer={0.15}
    why={`Each draw exceeds $Y$ with probability $q = 1-\\Phi\\left(\\frac{Y-\\mu}{\\sigma}\\right) = 1-\\Phi(1) \\approx 0.1587$. The count exceeding $Y$ is Binomial$(N,q)$: $P(k=2) = \\binom{5}{2}q^2(1-q)^3 = 10(0.1587)^2(0.8413)^3 \\approx 0.15$.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.34"
    company="Akuna Capital"
    prompt={`You pick three points independently and uniformly at random on a unit circle and form a triangle from them. What is the probability the triangle contains the circle's center?`}
    answer={0.25}
    why={`Fix the first point. The second point's angle $\\theta$ from the first is uniform on $(0, 2\\pi)$. Given $\\theta \\le \\pi$, the triangle contains the center only if the third point lands in a specific arc of length $\\pi - \\theta$, which happens with conditional probability $\\frac{\\pi-\\theta}{2\\pi}$. Averaging over $\\theta$: $\\frac1\\pi\\int_0^\\pi \\frac{\\pi-\\theta}{2\\pi}\\,d\\theta = \\frac14$.`}
  />
  <Problem
    title="5.35"
    company="Citadel"
    prompt={`A bag has $r=3$ red balls and $w=7$ white balls. You draw balls one at a time, without replacement, until the bag holds only one color. What is the probability that white balls run out first?`}
    answer={0.3}
    why={`White balls run out first exactly when the very last ball drawn (the $(r+w)$-th) is red — red only "survives" to the last draw if white was exhausted first. By symmetry, every ball is equally likely to be drawn last, so $P = r/(r+w) = 3/10 = 0.3$.`}
  />
