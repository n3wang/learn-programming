# Hard Disk Drives

Hard drives were the main persistent store for decades; file-system design still leans on their behavior [RW92, ADR03].

**Crux:** how do HDDs store and access data? What is the interface? How does scheduling help?

## Interface

A drive is an array of **sectors** (classically 512 B), addressed `0 … n−1`. Multi-sector I/O is common (e.g. 4 KB FS blocks), but manufacturers typically guarantee **atomicity only for a single sector** — larger writes can be **torn** on power loss.

**Unwritten contract** [SG04]: nearby LBAs are faster than distant ones; **sequential** access is fastest.

## Geometry

| Piece | Role |
|---|---|
| **Platter / surface** | Magnetic media (often 2 sides per platter) |
| **Spindle** | Spins at fixed RPM (e.g. 7.2K–15K) |
| **Track** | Concentric ring of sectors |
| **Head / arm** | Sense or write magnetism; seek across tracks |

**I/O time:** seek → rotational delay → transfer.

Seek phases: accelerate, coast, decelerate, **settle** (often 0.5–2 ms). Track **skew** keeps sequential I/O from missing the next sector after a track switch. **Multi-zone** disks put more sectors on outer tracks. On-drive **cache** (track buffer): reads may prefetch a track; writes may be **write-back** (fast, risky for ordered durability) or **write-through**.

## Doing the math

```text
T_I/O = T_seek + T_rotation + T_transfer
R_I/O = Size / T_I/O
```

Dimensional analysis helps: 10K RPM → 6 ms/rotation; half-rotation average ≈ 3 ms at 10K, 2 ms at 15K.

**Random vs sequential** (Cheetah- vs Barracuda-class numbers in the text): random 4 KB may be **~0.3–0.7 MB/s**; sequential approaches **~100+ MB/s** — often **200–300×** apart.

:::tip Use disks sequentially
Prefer sequential transfers, or at least large contiguous chunks. Tiny random I/O destroys performance.
:::

Average **seek distance** over all track pairs ≈ **⅓** of a full stroke (geometry integral) — that is where the “one-third” lore comes from.

## Disk scheduling

The scheduler estimates positioning cost and approximates **SJF**.

| Policy | Idea | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **SSTF / NBF** | Nearest track / nearest LBA next | **Starvation** of far requests |
| **SCAN / C-SCAN** | Elevator sweeps (F-SCAN freezes queue) | Fairer; still weak on rotation |
| **SPTF / SATF** | Minimize seek **+** rotation | Needs geometry; often **inside** the drive |

Modern OS: pick a **window** of good requests, **merge** adjacent LBAs, let the drive’s SPTF finish. Sometimes **wait** briefly for a better request (**anticipatory**, non-work-conserving) [ID01].

## Summary

| Idea | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| **Sector array** | Linear LBAs; sequential/nearby assumed fast |
| **Mechanics** | Seek + rotate + transfer |
| **Workloads** | Random ≪ sequential (huge gap) |
| **Schedulers** | SSTF → SCAN (fairness) → SPTF (rotation); merge + drive-internal reorder |
