> **Original interactive lesson for this course.**

Every digital circuit — from a single flip-flop to a whole CPU — is built out of a handful of **logic gates**, each computing a single boolean function of its inputs. A **truth table** just lists every possible input combination and the output for each.

## The basic gates

| Gate | Symbol | $Y=$ | 1 iff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BUFFER | pass-through | $Y=A$ | $A=1$ |
| NOT | inverter | $Y=\overline{A}$ | $A=0$ |
| AND | $\cdot$ | $Y=A\cdot B$ | both inputs are 1 |
| OR | $+$ | $Y=A+B$ | either input is 1 |
| NAND | AND then NOT | $Y=\overline{A\cdot B}$ | **not** both inputs are 1 |
| NOR | OR then NOT | $Y=\overline{A+B}$ | **neither** input is 1 |
| XOR | "exactly one" | $Y=A\oplus B$ | inputs differ |
| XNOR | "match" | $Y=\overline{A\oplus B}$ | inputs are the same |

NAND and NOR are called **universal gates** — every other gate (including NOT, AND, and OR themselves) can be built from nothing but NAND gates, or from nothing but NOR gates. That's not just a party trick: it's why a real chip fab only needs to mass-produce one or two gate shapes.

## Lab: build gates from a deck

Below, drag gates out of the **deck** on the left (search it if the list is long), drop them on the canvas, and wire them up — drag from an output dot to an input dot. Click an **input** card's circle to toggle it between 0 and 1 and watch the value propagate live. Once you think the circuit is right, click **Check**: it tests your circuit against the *entire* truth table, not just the wiring, so a circuit that's connected but logically wrong will still fail.

### Exercise 1 — NOT, using only a NAND gate

A NAND gate with both inputs tied to the same signal degenerates into a NOT gate: $\overline{A\cdot A}=\overline{A}$. Drag a single **NAND** onto the canvas, connect `A`'s output to **both** of its inputs, then connect its output to `Y`.

### Exercise 2 — XOR, using only AND, OR, and NAND

XOR isn't one of the universal gates, but it's easy to build from ones that are: $A\oplus B = (A+B)\cdot\overline{A\cdot B}$ — "at least one is on, AND they're not both on." Drag out one **OR**, one **NAND**, and one **AND**; wire both `A` and `B` into the OR *and* into the NAND, then feed the OR's output and the NAND's output into the AND, and the AND's output into `Y`.

If a check fails, the two revealed cases (after two attempts) show exactly which input combination your circuit gets wrong — a fast way to tell "not wired yet" (output shows `?`) from "wired, but the wrong gate" (output is a confident, wrong 0 or 1).
