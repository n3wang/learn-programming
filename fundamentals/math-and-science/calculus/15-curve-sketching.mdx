## Concavity

Intuitively: an arc is **concave upward** (a cup) or **concave downward** (a cap).

More precisely: near each $x_0$, if the graph lies **above** its tangent line, the arc is concave upward; if it lies **below** its tangent, concave downward. Real graphs often switch — down from $A$ to $B$, up from $B$ to $C$, down again from $C$ to $D$.

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-15-1-concavity.svg"
  alt="Fig. 15-1 cup, cap, and mixed concavity"
  caption="Fig. 15-1 — (a) cup (b) cap (c) changing concavity"
  maxWidth={420}
/>

The second derivative reads the shape:

### Theorem 15.1 (concavity)

(a) If $f''(x)>0$ on $(a,b)$, the graph is **concave upward** for $a<x<b$.

(b) If $f''(x)<0$ on $(a,b)$, the graph is **concave downward** for $a<x<b$.

<FormulaExplorer preset="concavitySign" />

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="concavity-from-second" />

### Examples

**(a)** $f(x)=x^{2}$: $f''=2>0$ everywhere → always concave up (upward parabola).

**(b)** $y=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}$ (upper unit semicircle). Implicitly $x+yy'=0$, then
$1+yy''+(y')^{2}=0$, so $y''=-[1+(y')^{2}]/y$. For $y>0$, $y''<0$ → always concave down.

## Points of inflection

An **inflection point** is where concavity **changes** (up on one side, down on the other). If $f''$ exists nearby and is continuous at $x_0$, a sign change forces $f''(x_0)=0$.

### Theorem 15.2

If the graph has an inflection at $x_0$, $f''$ exists in a neighborhood of $x_0$, and $f''$ is continuous at $x_0$, then $f''(x_0)=0$.

**Warning.** $f''(x_0)=0$ does **not** guarantee an inflection.

### Examples

| $f$ | $f''$ | Inflection? |
|---|---|---|
| $x^{3}$ | $6x$ | Yes at $0$ ($f''$ changes $-$ to $+$) |
| $x^{4}$ | $12x^{2}$ | **No** at $0$ ($f''\ge 0$ always) |
| $\tfrac13 x^{3}+\tfrac12 x^{2}-6x+8$ | $2x+1$ | Yes at $x=-\tfrac12$ |

## Vertical asymptotes

The line $x=x_0$ is a **vertical asymptote** if $f(x)\to+\infty$ or $-\infty$ as $x\to x_0$ from the left and/or the right.

For $f=g/h$ with $g,h$ continuous: if $h(x_0)=0$ and $g(x_0)\neq 0$, then $x=x_0$ is typically a vertical asymptote.

## Horizontal asymptotes

$y=y_0$ is a **horizontal asymptote** if $\lim_{x\to+\infty}f(x)=y_0$ or $\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=y_0$.

<FormulaExplorer preset="rationalAsymptotes" />

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="asymptotes" />

### Examples

| $f$ | Vertical | Horizontal |
|---|---|---|
| $1/x$ | $x=0$ | $y=0$ |
| $1/(x-2)$ | $x=2$ | $y=0$ |
| $(x-2)/((x-1)(x+3))$ | $x=1$, $x=-3$ | $y=0$ |
| $(x+4)/(x-3)$ | $x=3$ | $y=1$ |

## Symmetry

Two points $P,Q$ are **symmetric with respect to a line** $\ell$ if $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$. They are **symmetric with respect to a point** $B$ if $B$ is the midpoint of $PQ$.

A curve is symmetric wrt $\ell$ (or $B$) if every point on the curve has its partner on the curve. Then $\ell$ is an **axis of symmetry**.

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-15-2-symmetry-line.svg"
  alt="Fig. 15-2 symmetry with respect to a line"
  caption="Fig. 15-2 — reflection in a line; axis of symmetry"
  maxWidth={400}
/>

Coordinate facts:

- $(x,y)$ ↔ $(-x,y)$: symmetry in the **$y$-axis**
- $(x,y)$ ↔ $(x,-y)$: symmetry in the **$x$-axis**
- $(x,y)$ ↔ $(-x,-y)$: symmetry in the **origin**

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-15-3-axis-origin-symmetry.svg"
  alt="Fig. 15-3 axis and origin symmetry"
  caption="Fig. 15-3 — y-axis, x-axis, and origin reflections"
  maxWidth={420}
/>

For an equation $F(x,y)=0$:

1. **$y$-axis symmetry** iff $F(x,y)=0\Rightarrow F(-x,y)=0$
2. **$x$-axis symmetry** iff $F(x,y)=0\Rightarrow F(x,-y)=0$
3. **Origin symmetry** iff $F(x,y)=0\Rightarrow F(-x,-y)=0$

**Examples.** $y=x^{2}$ (even powers) → $y$-axis. $x=y^{2}$ → $x$-axis. Circles, ellipses, and standard hyperbolas are symmetric in both axes and the origin.

**Swap tip.** $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ are symmetric wrt the line $y=x$ (segment slope $-1$, midpoint on $y=x$).

## Inverse functions and symmetry

### Theorem 15.3

If $f$ is one-to-one with inverse $f^{-1}$, the graphs of $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are **symmetric to each other with respect to $y=x$**.

Reason: $(a,b)$ on $y=f(x)$ means $f(a)=b$, so $f^{-1}(b)=a$ — i.e. $(b,a)$ on the inverse graph.

**Examples.** $y=2x$ and $y=\tfrac12 x$; or $y=x^{2}$ (right branch as a function) reflected to $x=y^{2}$.

## Even and odd functions

- **Even:** $f(-x)=f(x)$ (domain symmetric) ⇔ graph symmetric in the **$y$-axis**
- **Odd:** $f(-x)=-f(x)$ ⇔ graph symmetric in the **origin**

Polynomials with only even powers are even; only odd powers are odd.

## Hints for sketching $y=f(x)$

1. Compute $y'$ (and $y''$ when convenient).
2. Critical numbers ($y'=0$ or undefined); classify extrema (1st/2nd derivative tests).
3. Mono intervals from the sign of $y'$.
4. Concavity from $y''$; check $y''=0$ / undefined for **inflection** (need a sign change).
5. **Vertical** asymptotes (e.g. poles of $g/h$).
6. **Horizontal** asymptotes ($\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}$).
7. End behavior if limits are $\pm\infty$.
8. $x$- and $y$-intercepts.
9. **Corners:** one-sided derivatives disagree finitely (e.g. $|x|$ at $0$).
10. **Cusps:** $y'\to+\infty$ or $-\infty$ from both sides (e.g. $\sqrt{|x|}$ at $0$).
11. **Oblique** asymptotes $y=mx+b$ with $f(x)-(mx+b)\to 0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="curve-sketch-checklist" />

:::important Key takeaways
- **$f''>0$** ⇒ concave up; **$f''<0$** ⇒ concave down.
- **Inflection** = concavity change; $f''=0$ is necessary (if continuous) but not sufficient.
- **Asymptotes:** vertical (blow-up), horizontal (limits at $\infty$), oblique (linear end behavior).
- **Even / odd** ↔ $y$-axis / origin symmetry; inverses reflect across $y=x$.
- Sketch with a fixed checklist: derivatives → extrema → mono → concavity → asymptotes → intercepts → special points.
:::
