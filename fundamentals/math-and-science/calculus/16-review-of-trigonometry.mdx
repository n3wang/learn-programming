## Angle measure

Degrees: a full turn is $360^\circ$. Calculus prefers **radians**.

On a circle of radius $1$ centered at $C$, take radii $CA$ and $CB$ so that arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ has length $1$. The central angle $\angle ACB$ measures **one radian**.

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-16-1-radian.svg"
  alt="Fig. 16-1 one radian on the unit circle"
  caption="Fig. 16-1 — arc length 1 on the unit circle defines 1 radian"
/>

Comparing to a full turn ($360^\circ$ vs circumference $2\pi$):

$$
1\text{ radian}=\frac{180}{\pi}\text{ degrees}\approx 57.3^\circ,
\qquad
1^\circ=\frac{\pi}{180}\text{ radians}.
$$

### Common conversions (corrected table)

| Degrees | Radians |
|---|---|
| $30$ | $\pi/6$ |
| $45$ | $\pi/4$ |
| $60$ | $\pi/3$ |
| $90$ | $\pi/2$ |
| $180$ | $\pi$ |
| $270$ | $3\pi/2$ |
| $360$ | $2\pi$ |

:::tip Book typo
Some printings list $180^\circ$ as $\pi/2$. A half turn is $\pi$ radians.
:::

### Arc length

On a circle of radius $r$, a central angle of $\theta$ **radians** cuts off arc length

$$
s=r\theta.
$$

(Reason: $\theta/(2\pi)=s/(2\pi r)$.)

## Sine and cosine (unit circle)

Place the origin at $O$ with $A=(1,0)$. Rotate ray $OA$ by a directed angle $\theta$ to tip $B$. Define:

1. $\cos\theta=$ the **$x$-coordinate** of $B$
2. $\sin\theta=$ the **$y$-coordinate** of $B$

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-16-6-unit-circle-def.svg"
  alt="Fig. 16-6 unit circle definition of sine and cosine"
  caption="Fig. 16-6 — (cos θ, sin θ) on the unit circle"
/>

### Examples

| $\theta$ | $B$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\sin\theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ or $2\pi$ | $(1,0)$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $\pi/2$ | $(0,1)$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| $\pi$ | $(-1,0)$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $3\pi/2$ | $(0,-1)$ | $0$ | $-1$ |

### Match with right-triangle ratios

For an acute angle, a similar right triangle with hypotenuse $1$ recovers

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}.
$$

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-16-7-triangle-def.svg"
  alt="Fig. 16-7 similar triangles for acute angles"
  caption="Fig. 16-7 — unit hypotenuse matches the classical ratios"

/>

### Special values

| $\theta$ | degrees | $\cos\theta$ | $\sin\theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0^\circ$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $\pi/6$ | $30^\circ$ | $\sqrt{3}/2$ | $1/2$ |
| $\pi/4$ | $45^\circ$ | $\sqrt{2}/2$ | $\sqrt{2}/2$ |
| $\pi/3$ | $60^\circ$ | $1/2$ | $\sqrt{3}/2$ |
| $\pi/2$ | $90^\circ$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| $\pi$ | $180^\circ$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $3\pi/2$ | $270^\circ$ | $0$ | $-1$ |

## Basic identities

**(16.1) Period $2\pi$:** $\cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta$, $\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta$.

**(16.2) Even / odd:** $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$.

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-16-8-even-odd.svg"
  alt="Fig. 16-8 cosine even sine odd"
  caption="Fig. 16-8 — reflection across the x-axis"
/>

**(16.3) Pythagorean:** $\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta=1$
(so $\sin^{2}\theta=1-\cos^{2}\theta$ and $\cos^{2}\theta=1-\sin^{2}\theta$).

**(16.4) Quadrant signs**

| I | $+$ | $+$ |
| II | $+$ | $-$ |
| III | $-$ | $-$ |

## Polar coordinates

**(16.5)** For a point $A(x,y)\neq O$, let $r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$ be the distance from the origin and $\theta$ the directed angle from the positive $x$-axis to ray $OA$. The pair $(r,\theta)$ are **polar coordinates** of $A$, and

$$
x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta.
$$

(Same as scaling the unit-circle point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ by $r$.)

## Angle-addition formulas

We take as a starting point (proved later / Problem 11 in the source notes):

**(16.6)** $\displaystyle \cos(u-v)=\cos u\cos v+\sin u\sin v$

Then quickly:

**(16.7)** $\cos(u+v)=\cos u\cos v-\sin u\sin v$  
(replace $v$ by $-v$ in (16.6); use cos even / sin odd).

**(16.8)** $\cos(\pi/2-v)=\sin v$ and $\sin(\pi/2-v)=\cos v$  
(set $u=\pi/2$ in (16.6); then swap).

**(16.9)** $\sin(u+v)=\sin u\cos v+\cos u\sin v$  
(write $\sin(u+v)=\cos\bigl(\pi/2-(u+v)\bigr)$ and expand).

**(16.10)** $\sin(u-v)=\sin u\cos v-\cos u\sin v$  
(replace $v$ by $-v$ in (16.9)).

## Double- and half-angle

**(16.11)** $\cos 2u=\cos^{2}u-\sin^{2}u=2\cos^{2}u-1=1-2\sin^{2}u$  
(set $v=u$ in (16.7); rewrite with $\sin^{2}+\cos^{2}=1$).

**(16.12)** $\sin 2u=2\sin u\cos u$  
(set $v=u$ in (16.9)).

**(16.13)** $\displaystyle \cos^{2}\!\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)=\frac{1+\cos u}{2}$  
(from $\cos u=2\cos^{2}(u/2)-1$).

**(16.14)** $\displaystyle \sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)=\frac{1-\cos u}{2}$  
(from Pythagorean + (16.13)).

## Laws of cosines and sines

**(16.15a) Law of Cosines.** In $\triangle ABC$ with side $c$ opposite angle $\theta$ (included by sides $a$ and $b$ in the usual labeling—or equivalently $c$ opposite $C$):

$$
c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos\theta.
$$

When $\theta=\pi/2$, this is Pythagoras.

**(16.15b) Law of Sines.**

$$
\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}=\frac{\sin C}{c}.
$$

:::important Key takeaways
- **Radians** for calculus: $1\text{ rad}=180/\pi$ degrees; arc length $s=r\theta$.
- **Unit circle:** $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ is the terminal point after rotating $(1,0)$ by $\theta$.
- **Polar:** $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$.
- **Core engine:** $\cos(u-v)$; then sums, cofunctions, $\sin(u\pm v)$, double/half-angle.
- **Triangles:** law of cosines $c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos\theta$; law of sines $\sin A/a=\cdots$.
:::
