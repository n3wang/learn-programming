## Relative maximum and minimum

A function $f$ has a **relative (local) maximum** at $x_0$ if

$$
f(x_0)\ge f(x)
$$

for every $x$ in some open interval around $x_0$ (where $f$ is defined). A **relative minimum** flips the inequality. Either is a **relative extremum**.

:::tip Local, not global
Relative extrema only compare nearby points. A local max need not be the tallest point on the whole graph.
:::

### Theorem — critical point

**If $f$ has a relative extremum at $x_0$ and $f'(x_0)$ exists, then $f'(x_0)=0$.**

So a differentiable local max/min has a **horizontal tangent**. (Nondifferentiable corners like $|x|$ at $0$ can still be extrema without a derivative.)

## Rolle’s theorem

**Rolle.** Let $f$ be continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, with $f(a)=f(b)=0$. Then there is at least one $x_0\in(a,b)$ with $f'(x_0)=0$.

Geometrically: if the graph hits the $x$-axis at both ends and is smooth in between, some interior tangent is horizontal (Fig. 13-2).

## Mean Value Theorem (Law of the Mean)

**MVT.** If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then some $x_0\in(a,b)$ satisfies

$$
\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}=f'(x_0).
$$

The left side is the **secant** (chord) slope between $P_1=(a,f(a))$ and $P_2=(b,f(b))$. The right side is a **tangent** slope. So somewhere the tangent is **parallel** to $P_1P_2$ (Fig. 13-4).

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-13-4-mvt.jpg"
  alt="Fig. 13-4 — MVT: an interior tangent parallel to the chord P1P2."
  caption="Fig. 13-4"
/>

This is also called the **Law of the Mean**.

<FormulaExplorer preset="meanValueSlope" />

### Extended / higher-order forms (preview)

If $f$ and $g$ are continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $g'\ne 0$ on $(a,b)$, then for some $x_0$

$$
\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(a)}=\frac{f'(x_0)}{g'(x_0)}.
$$

(The ordinary MVT is $g(x)=x$.)

A **higher-order** law of the mean expands $f(b)$ like a Taylor polynomial of degree $n-1$ about $a$, with remainder $\dfrac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}(b-a)^n$ for some $x_0$ between $a$ and $b$. Specializing $b\mapsto x$ or $a=0$ recovers the usual Taylor remainder forms.

## Increasing and decreasing functions

On an interval, $f$ is **increasing** if $u<v$ implies $f(u)<f(v)$, and **decreasing** if $u<v$ implies $f(u)>f(v)$.

### Theorem — sign of $f'$

- If $f'>0$ on an interval, then $f$ is **increasing** there.
- If $f'<0$ on an interval, then $f$ is **decreasing** there.

Proof sketch: for $u<v$, MVT gives $f(v)-f(u)=f'(c)(v-u)$ with the same sign as $f'(c)$.

:::important Key takeaways
- Differentiable **relative extrema** occur where $f'=0$ (horizontal tangent).
- **Rolle** / generalized Rolle: equal endpoint values ⇒ an interior horizontal tangent.
- **MVT (Law of the Mean):** average rate equals some instantaneous rate — tangent parallel to chord.
- **Sign of $f'$** decides increasing vs decreasing on an interval.
:::
