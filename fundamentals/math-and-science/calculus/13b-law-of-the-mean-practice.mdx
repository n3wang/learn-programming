## 14. Higher-order Law of the Mean (finish)

After defining $K$ so identity (2) holds and building the auxiliary $F$, Rolle (applied carefully) produces a point where $F'=0$. Differentiating $F$ makes intermediate terms **telescope**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F'(x_0)=&
f'(x_0)+\bigl[f''(x_0)(b-x_0)-f'(x_0)\bigr]
+\Bigl[\frac{f'''(x_0)}{2!}(b-x_0)^{2}-f''(x_0)(b-x_0)\Bigr]\\
&+\cdots+\Bigl[\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{(n-1)!}(b-x_0)^{n-1}-\frac{f^{(n-1)}(x_0)}{(n-2)!}(b-x_0)^{n-2}\Bigr]
-Kn(b-x_0)^{n-1}\\
=&
\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{(n-1)!}(b-x_0)^{n-1}-Kn(b-x_0)^{n-1}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $K=\dfrac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}$, and (2) becomes the higher-order mean-value formula (1).

## 15. Vanishing derivative ⇒ constant

**Claim.** If $f'=0$ on $(a,b)$, then $f$ is constant on $(a,b)$.

**Idea.** For $u<v$ in $(a,b)$, MVT gives $\dfrac{f(v)-f(u)}{v-u}=f'(x_0)=0$, so $f(v)=f(u)$.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="prove-constant-from-derivative" />

:::tip Problem 18
If $f'=g'$ on $(a,b)$, then $(f-g)'=0$, so Problem 15 yields $f=g+K$.
:::
