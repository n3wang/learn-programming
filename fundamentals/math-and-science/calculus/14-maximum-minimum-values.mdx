## Critical numbers

A number $x_0$ in the domain of $f$ is a **critical number** if either

$$
f'(x_0)=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad f'(x_0)\text{ is undefined}.
$$

From Theorem 13.1: a **differentiable** relative extremum must occur at a critical number ($f'=0$). The converse fails — e.g. $f(x)=x^{3}$ has $f'(0)=0$ but neither a relative max nor min at $0$.

### Examples

| $f$ | $f'$ | Critical numbers |
|---|---|---|
| $7x^{2}-3x+5$ | $14x-3$ | $\dfrac{3}{14}$ |
| $x^{3}-2x^{2}+x+1$ | $3x^{2}-4x+1$ | $1$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ |
| $x^{2/3}$ | $\dfrac{2}{3}x^{-1/3}$ | $0$ ($f'$ undefined) |

## Second derivative test

Assume $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f''(x_0)$ exists.

| $f''(x_0)$ | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| $<0$ | relative **maximum** |
| $>0$ | relative **minimum** |
| $=0$ | **inconclusive** |

When $f''(x_0)=0$, anything can happen: $x^{4}$ (min), $-x^{4}$ (max), $x^{3}$ (neither) — all have $f''(0)=0$.

### Examples

- $f(x)=7x^{2}-3x+5$: critical $\dfrac{3}{14}$, $f''=14>0$ → relative **min**.
- $f(x)=x^{3}-2x^{2}+x+1$: $f''=6x-4$; at $1$ get $+2$ (min), at $\dfrac{1}{3}$ get $-2$ (max).
- $f(x)=x^{2/3}$: critical $0$ with $f'$ undefined → second-derivative test **N/A**.

## First derivative test

Useful when $f''(x_0)=0$, $f''$ is hard, or you prefer sign charts. Assume $f'(x_0)=0$ (or a critical number where a sign chart still makes sense).

| Signs of $f'$ left → right of $x_0$ | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| $\{+,-\}$ | relative **maximum** |
| $\{-,+\}$ | relative **minimum** |
| $\{+,+\}$ or $\{-,-\}$ | **neither** |

### Example — when $f''=0$

At $0$ for $x^{4}$, $-x^{4}$, $x^{3}$:

- $4x^{3}$: $\{-,+\}$ → relative **min**
- $-4x^{3}$: $\{+,-\}$ → relative **max**
- $3x^{2}$: $\{+,+\}$ → **neither** (inflection)

## Absolute maximum and minimum

On a set $S$, an **absolute maximum** at $x_0\in S$ means $f(x)\le f(x_0)$ for all $x\in S$; absolute minimum flips the inequality.

### Tabular method on $[a,b]$

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, the Extreme Value Theorem guarantees abs max/min. Find them by:

1. Critical numbers $c_i$ in $(a,b)$
2. Table: $a$, each $c_i$, and $b$
3. Evaluate $f$ — **largest** value = abs max; **smallest** = abs min

### Example

$f(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-x+2$ on $[0,2]$.

$f'(x)=(3x+1)(x-1)$ → critical numbers $-\dfrac{1}{3}$ (outside) and $1$ (inside).

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
|---|---|
| $0$ | $2$ |
| $1$ | $1$ |
| $2$ | $4$ |

Absolute **maximum** $4$ at $x=2$; absolute **minimum** $1$ at $x=1$.

  solution={`def abs_extrema(pairs):
    max_x, max_v = pairs[0]
    min_x, min_v = pairs[0]
    for x, y in pairs[1:]:
        if y > max_v or (y == max_v and x < max_x):
            max_v, max_x = y, x
        if y < min_v or (y == min_v and x < min_x):
            min_v, min_x = y, x
    return (max_v, max_x, min_v, min_x)

print(abs_extrema([(0, 2), (1, 1), (2, 4)]))
`}

## Unique relative extremum ⇒ absolute

**Theorem.** If continuous $f$ on an interval $J$ (finite or infinite) has a **unique** relative extremum in $J$, that extremum is also absolute on $J$.

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-14-4-unique-relative-is-absolute.jpg"
  alt="Fig. 14-4 unique relative maximum is absolute"
  caption="Fig. 14-4 — a lone relative max is absolute on the interval"
/>

Idea: if some $d$ beat the unique relative max at $c$, EVT on the segment between $c$ and $d$ would force another interior extremum — contradiction. For a unique relative min, apply the result to $-f$.

---

:::important Key takeaways
- **Critical numbers:** $f'=0$ or $f'$ undefined — candidates for relative extrema.
- **Second-derivative test:** sign of $f''(c)$ when $f'(c)=0$; inconclusive if $f''(c)=0$.
- **First-derivative test:** sign patterns $\{+,-\}$, $\{-,+\}$, same-sign.
- **Absolute on $[a,b]$:** compare $f$ at endpoints and interior critical numbers.
- A **unique** relative extremum on an interval is absolute there.
:::
