# 20. Related Rates

> **Adapted from:** Classical single-variable calculus (time rates linked by a geometric or algebraic equation). Rewritten for teaching — not a verbatim textbook dump.

If a quantity $y$ depends on time $t$, its rate of change is $dy/dt$. When **several** time-dependent quantities are tied together by an equation, differentiate that equation with respect to $t$ to relate their rates.

## The method

1. **Draw** a figure; introduce variables for every quantity that **changes** with time.
2. Write an equation that relates those variables (geometry, similar triangles, volume formulas, …).
3. Identify which rates are known and which rate is asked for.
4. Differentiate **both sides with respect to $t$** (Chain Rule). Constants stay constant.
5. **Then** plug in the values at the specific instant of interest; solve for the unknown rate.
6. Interpret the sign (e.g. negative $dy/dt$ may mean “sliding down”).

:::tip Timing
Substitute numerical values **after** differentiating, unless a quantity is truly constant for all $t$ (like a fixed ladder length).
:::

## Example 20.1 — Sliding ladder

A $25$-foot ladder leans against a vertical wall. The bottom slides away from the wall at $3$ ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is $7$ ft from the wall?

<LessonFigure
  src="/img/calculus/fig-20-1-ladder.svg"
  alt="Fig. 20-1 sliding ladder"
  caption="Fig. 20-1 — x² + y² = 625"
  maxWidth={300}
/>

Let $x$ = distance of the bottom from the wall, $y$ = height of the top. Given $dx/dt=3$; find $dy/dt$ when $x=7$.

Pythagoras:

$$
x^{2}+y^{2}=25^{2}=625. \tag{20.1}
$$

Differentiate in $t$:

$$
2x\frac{dx}{dt}+2y\frac{dy}{dt}=0
\implies
x\frac{dx}{dt}+y\frac{dy}{dt}=0. \tag{20.2}
$$

At $x=7$: $49+y^{2}=625$ ⇒ $y=24$ (positive height). With $dx/dt=3$:

$$
7\cdot 3+24\cdot\frac{dy}{dt}=0
\implies
\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{7}{8}.
$$

So the top is sliding **down** at $\dfrac{7}{8}$ ft/s.

:::important Key takeaways
- Related rates: one equation among time-varying quantities → differentiate in $t$ → solve for the unknown rate
- Plug the specific instant **after** differentiating
- Sign of the rate answers “up or down / in or out”
- Ladder model: $x^{2}+y^{2}=L^{2}$ ⇒ $dy/dt=-(x/y)\\,dx/dt$
:::
