# 10.1 Wavelet analysis

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §10.1. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [9.8](./09-08-fourier-code-sketches). Next: [10.1b](./10-01b-wavelet-intro-labs).

## Part I — when Fourier is not enough

Chapter 9 told you **which** frequencies sit in a record. Many real signals also change **when** those frequencies appear: brain rhythms, markets, gravitational-wave chirps, image textures. Part I of this chapter builds **wavelets** — localized oscillating packets. Part II (later) covers **principal components** for huge, correlated space–time data.

## The pedagogical signal

![Figure 10.1 — multi-tone signal with STFT window boxes.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-1-multitone-signal.svg)

$$
y(t)=
\begin{cases}
\sin 2\pi t, & 0\le t\le 2,\\
5\sin 2\pi t+10\sin 4\pi t, & 2\le t\le 8,\\
2.5\sin 2\pi t+6\sin 4\pi t+10\sin 6\pi t, & 8\le t\le 12.
\end{cases}
$$

A global DFT mixes all three regimes into one spectrum: every harmonic looks “always on.” You want a compact picture of **which band is active at which time**.

Fourier bases are infinite-support sinusoids; their overlaps store correlated, redundant information — awkward for compression. Wavelets are **wave packets**: finite support (or rapid decay) in time, hence a finite band in frequency. Expand $y$ in a complete family of such packets, each centered at a different time.

![Figure 10.2 — Morlet, Mexican hat, Haar, Daubechies-like mothers.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-2-mother-wavelets.svg)

## Mother → daughters

A **mother** wavelet $\Psi(t)$ is one localized shape. **Daughters** come from scale $a$ and translate $\tau$. Pick the daughter formula (and why a plain DFT fails the staged multi-tone) among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="wavelet-mother-daughter" />

Examples of mothers:

- **Morlet:** $\Psi(t)=e^{2\pi i t}e^{-t^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ (oscillating Gaussian).
- **Mexican hat:** $\Psi(t)=(1-t^2/\sigma^2)e^{-t^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ (second derivative of a Gaussian).
- **Haar:** a simple $+1/-1$ step.
- **Daubechies:** compact, fractal-looking orthogonal filters (details later).

All are large only for a while and carry a limited frequency range — hence joint time–frequency resolution (subject to an uncertainty bound, §10.2).
