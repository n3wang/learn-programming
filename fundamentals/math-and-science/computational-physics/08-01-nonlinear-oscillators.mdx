# 8.1 Nonlinear oscillators

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §8.1. Rewritten in original wording — not a paste of textbook figures or listings. Continues [7.8 Code-listing sketches](./07-06-code-listing-sketches). Next: [8.2–8.3 ODE review](./08-02-ode-form-and-review).

## Why leave the harmonic world?

Every first course solves $m x''=-kx$. Real springs, pendula at large angles, and molecular bonds leave that paradise. The computational story of this chapter is: **write Newton’s law**, **cast it as a first-order system**, and **integrate with a controlled ODE algorithm** while watching energy and period.

![Figure 8.1 — mass–spring with an optional external drive (hand).](/img/computational-physics/fig-8-1-mass-spring-hand.svg)

Newton’s second law for one coordinate, then two potentials with different restoring forces. Pick each equation among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="nonlinear-oscillator-models" />

For small $|x|$ the soft model recovers $-kx$. Past $x>1/\alpha$ the “spring” no longer restores. Only **$p=2$** in the power-law family is harmonic (isochronous); other even $p$ stay periodic in a bound well but with amplitude-dependent period.

![Figure 8.2 — harmonic vs soft anharmonic $V(x)$, and $|x|^p$ wells.](/img/computational-physics/fig-8-2-potentials.svg)

## Physics takeaways

- Bound motion in a 1D well is periodic (return to the same $(x,v)$).
- Isochronous (amplitude-independent period) only for the harmonic case $p=2$.
- Soft $\alpha$ models can cease to be bound if the particle climbs past $x=1/\alpha$.
- External drives and friction come later (§8.6); first we need ODE machinery.
