# 6.8 Nonlinear fit to a resonance

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §6.8. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [6.6b Least-squares fitting labs](./06-03b-exponential-decay-least-squares-labs). Labs: [6.8b Nonlinear least-squares labs](./06-04b-nonlinear-resonance-fit-labs).

## When least squares stops being linear algebra

Back in [6.5](./06-01-lagrange-interpolation) the neutron cross-section table (Table 6.1) was interpolated without ever using the theory behind it — the Breit–Wigner resonance formula

$$
f(E)=\frac{f_r}{(E-E_r)^2+\Gamma^2/4}.
$$

Now the goal is to actually **fit** this formula to the data: find the values of $E_r$ (resonance energy), $f_r$ (peak height), and $\Gamma$ (width) that minimize $\chi^2$. Because $f(E)$ is **not** linear in these three parameters, the $\chi^2$-minimization conditions $\partial\chi^2/\partial a_m=0$ from [6.6](./06-03-exponential-decay-least-squares) become nonlinear simultaneous equations — the closed-form linear-regression formulas no longer apply.

## Rewriting the theory in fit-parameter notation

Relabel $a_1=f_r$, $a_2=E_r$, $a_3=\Gamma^{2}/4$ and $x=E$. The model, its three partials $\partial g/\partial a_m$, and the nonlinear residuals $f_1=f_2=f_3=0$ follow — pick each equation among lookalikes.

## Newton-Raphson in three dimensions

Linearize the three residuals about a guess $\mathbf a$, build the Jacobian by forward differences, and update $\mathbf a\leftarrow\mathbf a+\Delta\mathbf a$. Pick each equation among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="nonlinear-resonance-fit" />

## Practical notes on the search

- **Starting guesses matter.** Because $f_r,E_r,\Gamma$ each have direct physical meaning (peak height, peak location, full width at half maximum), a rough graph of the data gives good starting values — much better than an arbitrary guess.
- **Local vs. global minima.** Newton-Raphson only guarantees convergence to *a* root, not necessarily the best one. Repeat the search from a grid of different starting guesses; if different searches converge to different minima, keep the one with the lowest $\chi^2$.
- **Ill-conditioning is common.** Parameters that affect the fit weakly (or that are strongly correlated with another parameter) make the Jacobian nearly singular, producing wild, unstable correction steps. This is a real hazard of nonlinear fitting, not just a bug — backtracking (accepting only a fraction of $\Delta\mathbf a$ per step) and better initial guesses both help.
