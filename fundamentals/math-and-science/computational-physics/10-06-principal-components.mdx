# 10.6 Part II: Principal components analysis

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §10.6. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [10.5b](./10-05b-dwt-labs). Next: [10.6b](./10-06b-pca-labs).

## Why PCA after Fourier / wavelets

Fourier bases are infinite, correlated, and awkward to truncate. Wavelets compress 1D / image-like signals well but are a poor fit for **huge, unlabeled, multivariate** tables (stellar spectra, EEG arrays, faces, ocean fields). **PCA** rotates the measurement axes into **principal components** that point along directions of maximal variance (“power”) in dataspace — unsupervised dimensionality reduction, or equivalently: extract the dominant dynamics in a noisy, redundant cloud.

Same idea as principal axes for rigid-body inertia: choose coordinates where the description is simplest.

## Iris warm-up

Four features (cm): sepal length/width, petal length/width. A short set of samples (subset of the classic iris table) is enough to practice means, variances, and covariances before full eigen-PCA.

## Data space $\mathcal{R}^M$

![Figure 10.13 — multiple detectors on a beam.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-13-detectors.svg)

At each instant, $M$ observables form one sample vector (e.g. eight coordinates from four detectors’ $(x,y)$). Thousands of times × many channels ⇒ enormous $N\times M$ tables, often noisy and correlated across detectors.

## Variance and SNR

For a scalar channel with $N$ samples,

$$
\bar z=\frac1N\sum_i z_i,\qquad
\sigma^2(z)=\mathrm{Var}(z)=\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(z_i-\bar z)^2.
$$

Signal-to-noise ratio

$$
\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{signal}}^2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}^2}.
$$

PCA’s working hypothesis: **largest variance ≈ most interesting dynamics**; orthogonal residual directions may be noise or weaker modes.

![Figure 10.14 — PC₁ along max variance; PC₂ secondary; right: projected cloud.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-14-pca-axes.svg)

## Covariance matrix

Center channels $a_i=a_i'-\bar a$, $b_i=b_i'-\bar b$. Pick the sample covariance and the matrix form $C=\mathbf X\mathbf X^{T}/(N-1)$ among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="pca-variance-covariance" />

Positive cov ⇒ channels tend to move together; large |cov| ⇒ redundancy; zero ⇒ uncorrelated (for this linear measure). Stack $M$ centered detector rows into an $M\times N$ matrix $\mathbf X$ (row = all times for one channel; column = one time across channels).

- Diagonal $C_{ii}$: variances of individual channels.
- Off-diagonal $C_{ij}$: correlations between channels $i$ and $j$.
- $C$ is square, symmetric, and positive semidefinite.

## PCA pipeline (2D demo)

![Figures 10.15–16 — cloud, eigenvectors, PCA coordinates.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-15-16-pca-demo.svg)

Classic steps (Smith-style $N=10$ points in the labs):

1. **Enter** the $N\times 2$ (or $M\times N$) table.
2. **Center** each feature (subtract column/row means).
3. **Build** $C$ (for 2D: $\mathrm{var}\,x$, $\mathrm{cov}(x,y)$, $\mathrm{var}\,y$).
4. **Eigendecompose** $C$: eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf{PC}_1,\mathbf{PC}_2,\ldots$. The largest $\lambda$ is the principal direction — often most of the power ($\lambda_1/(\sum\lambda)$).
5. **Project**: form a feature matrix $F$ whose columns (or rows) are the kept PCs; compute $X^{\mathrm{PCA}}=F^{T} X^{T}$ (convention: match your layout). Keeping only $\mathbf{PC}_1$ collapses the cloud onto a line; two PCs recover a rotated plane.

### Demonstration numbers (2D)

For the Smith cloud (labs table), after centering you should recover something like

$$
C\approx\begin{bmatrix}0.6166&0.6154\\0.6154&0.7166\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\lambda_1\approx 1.284,\quad
\lambda_2\approx 0.049,
\quad
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}\approx 96\%,
$$

with unit $\mathbf{PC}_1\approx(-0.678,-0.735)$ (sign is conventional — flip both components together). $\mathbf{PC}_2$ is orthogonal and carries the leftover few percent. Projecting a centered sample $\mathbf r$ is the score $\mathbf{PC}_1\cdot\mathbf r$ (and optionally $\mathbf{PC}_2\cdot\mathbf r$).

Iris remains your open problem: same pipeline in $M=4$. Chaotic-pendulum phase-space samples (Chapter 8) make a nice physics follow-on — store $\sim 10$ cycles past transients and PCA the $(\theta,\omega)$ (or higher) table.

## Takeaway

1. Center features.
2. Form $C=\frac1{N-1}XX^{T}$.
3. Eigendecompose → ordered PCs.
4. Project onto the top $k$ PCs (drop small-$\lambda$ “noise” directions).
