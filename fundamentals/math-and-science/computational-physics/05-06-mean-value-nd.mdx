# 5.6 Mean-value theorem and high-D Monte Carlo

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §5.6. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [5.5 Monte Carlo integration](./05-05-monte-carlo-integration). Labs: [5.6b Mean-value and high-D labs](./05-06b-mean-value-nd-labs).

## Mean-value form

For a continuous $f$ on $[a,b]$ there is some $c\in[a,b]$ with

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=(b-a)\,f(c).
$$

Monte Carlo replaces the unknown $f(c)$ by a **sample mean**. Draw $x_i$ uniformly from $[a,b]$ and set

$$
\langle f\rangle\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} f(x_i),
\qquad
I\simeq (b-a)\langle f\rangle.
$$

Equivalently, every sample carries the same weight $w_i=(b-a)/N$:

$$
I\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{N} f(x_i)\,w_i.
$$

![Figure 5.6 — mean height of random samples times width approximates the area.](/img/computational-physics/fig-5-6-mean-value.svg)

If the sample variance of the $f(x_i)$ is $\sigma_f^{2}$, the variance of the mean scales as $\sigma_f^{2}/N$, so

$$
\sigma_I\sim\frac{\sigma_f}{\sqrt{N}}\,(b-a)
$$

(up to the usual $(b-a)$ factor already folded into $I$). Doubling accuracy costs about $4\times$ more samples — slow but predictable.

## Why high dimensions favor Monte Carlo

A conventional product grid with $64$ points per axis in $D=36$ would need $64^{36}$ evaluations — not a serious plan. Monte Carlo still needs only $N$ integrand calls, and the leading error remains $\sim 1/\sqrt{N}$ **independent of $D$** (for plain independent sampling). Rough crossover lore: around $D\approx 3$–$4$, Monte Carlo starts competing with simple deterministic rules; for larger $D$ it usually wins on cost-per-digit.

## A 10-dimensional check

A textbook calibration (smooth, known exact value) is

$$
I=\int_{[0,1]^{10}}\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{10} x_i\Biggr)^{2}d^{10}x=\frac{155}{6}.
$$

One Monte Carlo trial: draw $N$ points in the unit hypercube, average $f(\mathbf{x})=(\sum x_i)^{2}$. Averaging several independent seeds reduces run-to-run jitter. Relative error $|I_{\mathrm{MC}}-155/6|/(155/6)$ should shrink like $1/\sqrt{N}$ as you raise $N$ or average trials.

## Two-dimensional generalization

On a rectangle $[a,b]\times[c,d]$,

$$
\int_a^b\int_c^d f(x,y)\,dy\,dx=(b-a)(d-c)\,\langle f\rangle,
$$

with $(x_i,y_i)$ uniform in the rectangle. The same pattern extends to a hyper-rectangle of volume $V$: $I\simeq V\langle f\rangle$.
