# 8.5 Nonlinear oscillation solutions

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §8.5. Original wording. Continues [8.4b](./08-03b-ode-algorithms-labs). Labs: [8.5b](./08-04b-nonlinear-oscillation-labs).

## Same stepper, new force

Once $(x,v)$ is in dynamic form, swapping potentials is a one-line change in $\mathbf f$:

$$
F=-k x^{p-1}
\quad(p\text{ even; odd power of }x\text{ restores}),
$$

or the soft model $F=-kx(1-\alpha x)$. Integrate with RK2/RK4 exactly as for the harmonic case.

![Mass–spring / anharmonic potentials.](/img/computational-physics/fig-8-2-potentials.svg)

## What changes physically

- **Period vs amplitude.** For $p\neq 2$, larger energy ⇒ different period. Measure $T$ by detecting successive $x=0$ crossings with $v>0$ (or peaks of $x$).
- **Energy still checks the ODE.** For autonomous conservative $F(x)$, $E=\tfrac12 mv^{2}+V(x)$ should stay flat if the algorithm is accurate — even when $V$ is not $\tfrac12 kx^{2}$.
- **Soft $\alpha$.** Start with small amplitude; push past $x\sim 1/\alpha$ and watch escape.

Slide amplitude and softness below: the measured period stretches away from the harmonic $T_0=2\pi$ as $A\alpha$ grows.

## Harmonic place-markers

Keep the $p=2$ benchmark ($\omega=2\pi$, $T=1$) beside every nonlinear run: same IC, same $h$, compare waveforms and energy drift. Nonlinear labs lock one RK2 micro-step on $F=-x^{3}$ so the force wiring is unambiguous before long integrations.

## Takeaway

| Model | Expect |
| --- | --- |
| $F=-kx$ | $T$ independent of $A$ |
| Soft / hard $F(x)$ | $T=T(A)$ |
| Any conservative $F(x)$ | flat $E$ if the stepper is good |
