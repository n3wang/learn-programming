# 12.10 Shor’s factoring algorithm

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §12.10. Original wording — **no** `Amod15` / Qiskit dump. Continues [12.9b](./12-09b-grover-labs). Labs: [12.10b](./12-10b-shor-labs).

RSA-style cryptology leans on the hardness of factoring large integers. **Shor’s algorithm** finds nontrivial factors of $N$ in (believed) polynomial time on a fault-tolerant quantum computer by reducing factoring to **period finding**.

## Classical outline

1. Pick random $1<r<N$.  
2. $K=\gcd(r,N)$; if $K\neq 1$, $K$ is a factor — done.  
3. Find the smallest period $T$ of $f(x)=r^x\bmod N$.  
4. If $T$ is odd or $r^{T/2}\equiv-1\pmod N$, retry with a new $r$.  
5. Else $\gcd(r^{T/2}\pm 1,\,N)$ yields nontrivial factors.

Example toy: $N=15$. A successful run might draw $r=8$, read phase $0.25$ ⇒ $T=4$, then recover factors **3** and **5**.

## Modular multiplication unitary

A unitary $U$ (for $r$ coprime to $N$) multiplies the computational register by $r$ mod $N$. Eigenstates $|u_S\rangle$ carry eigenvalues $e^{2\pi i S/T}$; estimating that phase reveals $S/T$. Pick $U|y\rangle$ and the $T=1/\phi$ reading among lookalikes in the guided card below.

## Phase estimation

For unitary $U|u\rangle=e^{2\pi i\phi}|u\rangle$, a **$t$-qubit** control register plus Hadamards and controlled-$U^{2^j}$ kicks phases onto the controls. An **inverse QFT** then reads an integer $s\approx 2^t\phi$, so $\phi\approx s/2^t$.

![Figure 12.13 — phase estimation.](/img/computational-physics/fig-12-13-phase-est.svg)

## Period finding → factors

Prepare the target register in $|1\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt T}\sum_S|u_S\rangle$. Phase estimation samples $\phi=S/T$; **continued fractions** recover denominator $T$. Then apply the gcd steps above.

## Takeaway

| Step | Tool |
| --- | --- |
| Random $r$, gcd | classical |
| Period of $r^x\bmod N$ | QPE + modular $U$ |
| $T\to$ factors | $\gcd(r^{T/2}\pm1,N)$ |
| QFT link | inverse QFT inside QPE |
