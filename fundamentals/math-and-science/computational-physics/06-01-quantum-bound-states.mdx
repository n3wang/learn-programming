# 6.1 Quantum bound states

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), Ch. 6 intro–§6.1. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [5.9 Integration reference sketches](./05-09-integration-reference-sketches). Next algorithm: [6.2 Bisection search](./06-02-bisection-search).

Chapter 6 is about **searching** and **fitting**. Many physics problems do not hand you $x$ explicitly — they hand you a nonlinear condition $f(x)=0$ (or a table of measurements) and ask you to recover a parameter. Two themes run through the chapter:

1. **Trial-and-error search** — guess a value, evaluate a residual, improve the guess (bisection, Newton, …).
2. **Fitting** — later sections interpolate or fit tabulated data (Lagrange §6.5, splines §6.6).

The first concrete search problem is a finite square well: find the allowed energies by solving a transcendental matching condition.

## The square-well eigenvalue problem

A particle in a finite square well of depth $V_0$ and half-width $a$ has even and odd bound stationary states. Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at the wall produces a transcendental equation for the bound energy $E$.

Work in the book's convenient units

$$
\hbar=1,\qquad 2m=1,\qquad a=1,\qquad V_0=10,
$$

Bound energies sit below the continuum; write $E=-E_B$ with binding energy $0<E_B<V_0$. In what follows $E$ means that positive $E_B$ (book notation), so $0<E<V_0=10$. Define

$$
\kappa=\sqrt{V_0-E}=\sqrt{10-E}.
$$

**Even** parity states obey

$$
\sqrt{10-E}\,\tan\sqrt{10-E}=\sqrt{E},
$$

or the algebraically equivalent residual (same zeros, fewer $\tan$ poles to trip over)

$$
g(E)=\sqrt{E}\,\cot\sqrt{10-E}-\sqrt{10-E}=0.
$$

**Odd** parity uses the companion matching condition

$$
\sqrt{10-E}\,\cot\sqrt{10-E}=\sqrt{E},
$$

or the residual form (same zeros)

$$
g_{\mathrm{odd}}(E)=\sqrt{E}\,\tan\sqrt{10-E}-\sqrt{10-E}=0.
$$

(Even used $\cot$; odd flips to $\tan$ — poles sit in different places, so pick a fresh bracket.)

The numerical job is the same: find roots of a continuous residual on a safe bracket. There is no closed-form $E$ — evaluate $g$ at trial energies and hunt for sign changes (bisection in §6.2).

Drag $E$ in the explorer until the even residual crosses zero — that energy is a candidate bound state for $V_0=10$.

## A safe even-root bracket

For $V_0=10$, the even residual $g(E)$ changes sign on

$$
[8.0,\,8.8]:\qquad g(8.0)<0,\quad g(8.8)>0.
$$

Halving that interval until the width is tiny yields the bound energy

$$
E_{B}\approx 8.5927852752.
$$

(Use $\cot\theta=\cos\theta/\sin\theta$ with `math.sin` / `math.cos`. Near $\sin\kappa=0$ the residual has poles — stay inside a bracket that already has opposite signs.)

Deepening the well to $V_0=20$ adds more even roots (near $\sim 6.11$ and $\sim 18.36$). The same $g(E)$ with $10\to 20$ and a fresh bracket finds them.

## Takeaway

| Parity | Matching idea | Residual to root-find |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Even | $\kappa\tan\kappa=\sqrt{E}$ | $g=\sqrt{E}\,\cot\kappa-\kappa$ |
| Odd | $\kappa\cot\kappa=\sqrt{E}$ | $g_{\mathrm{odd}}=\sqrt{E}\,\tan\kappa-\kappa$ |
| Either | No closed $E$ | Bracket → bisect (§6.2) → check $\|g\|$ |

Here $\kappa=\sqrt{V_0-E}$ (with $V_0=10$ in the explorer).
