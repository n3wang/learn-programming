# 11.1 Part I: Biological and artificial neural networks

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), Ch. 11 opener + §11.1. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [10.7b](./10-07b-wavelet-code-labs). Next: [11.1b](./11-01b-neural-networks-intro-labs).

> Automated systems should provide explanations that are technically valid, meaningful and useful… calibrated to the level of risk based on the context.
> — White House AI Blueprint (2022)

## Why this chapter

Brains (≈ $10^{11}$ neurons) solve pattern problems that hard-coded programs struggle with. **AI** aims to capture that tacit skill; **machine learning (ML)** trains neural nets from labeled examples; **deep learning** stacks many layers; **generative** setups pair a generator with an evaluator. Physics demos here stay deliberately small so you can see the innards.

**Problem (chapter arc):** model a neuron and a network of neurons, and check whether the net can learn.

## Biological neuron

![Figure 11.1 — dendrites, soma, axon, synapses.](/img/computational-physics/fig-11-1-neuron.svg)

Dendrites collect excitatory / inhibitory pulses. The cell body integrates them; if a threshold is crossed it fires an **action potential** along the axon (roughly $0$–$30\,\mathrm{mV}$ negative, $1$–$6\,\mathrm{ms}$ wide) — fire or not, then rest. Cortex stacks layers hierarchically (senses → outer layers → deeper maps → response such as face recognition). Biological nets are highly parallel and surprisingly robust: no single clique is usually indispensable.

## Artificial nets (McCulloch–Pitts → Perceptron)

McCulloch & Pitts (1943) gave a mathematical neuron and proved nets can be trained. Rosenblatt’s **Perceptron** (1957) showed learning on an IBM 704 (e.g. left- vs right-marked cards) by tweaking connections after each trial — sensational then, underpowered by 1950s machines.

An artificial net routes data through **nodes** (neurons). Each node takes several inputs, computes with changeable parameters, and — if criteria are met — sends an output along an edge. Learning = iteratively updating those parameters from prediction error.

## Takeaway

| Idea | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Biological neuron | Integrate → threshold → spike |
| Artificial node | Weighted sum + activation + bias |
| Learning | Change weights/biases from error |
| Deep net | Many hidden layers |
