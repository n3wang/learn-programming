# 7.5 Two masses on a string

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §7.5. Rewritten in original wording — self-contained reduced model, not a paste of a missing §7.1 listing. Continues [7.4b Tests-before-use labs](./07-02b-tests-before-use-labs). Labs: [7.5b String-problem labs](./07-03b-string-problem-labs).

## From statics to $\mathbf f(\mathbf x)=\mathbf 0$

Two masses hanging from three string segments give a classic statics puzzle: horizontal and vertical force balance at each mass, plus geometric constraints on the angles. The unknowns are tensions $T_1,T_2,T_3$ and angles $\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3$ — six equations, nonlinear in the trig functions.

That full system is a fine ultimate target, but it is too many unknowns for a first nonlinear lab. The computational point of the chapter is smaller and sharper:

- Physics produces a **nonlinear** system $\mathbf f(\mathbf x)=\mathbf 0$.
- Identities like $\cos\theta=\pm\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\theta}$ reduce the dimension — and the **sign** selects a geometric branch.
- Multidimensional **Newton** (Jacobian $J$, solve $J\Delta\mathbf x=-\mathbf f$) finds a root from a fair guess.
- Physical filters reject algebraic roots: $T>0$, $|\sin|,|\cos|\le 1$, and a sketch that matches the apparatus.

## Sign of the cosine

Write $s=\sin\theta$ and $c=\cos\theta$. Replacing $\cos$ by $\pm\sqrt{1-s^{2}}$ drops an unknown — but the $\pm$ encodes which side of vertical the string leans. The guided walk below makes that identity and the toy $f_1,f_2$ system pickable among lookalikes.

## A self-contained Newton toy

To practice the same algebra without six coupled statics equations, use a two-unknown nonlinear system (unit circle ∩ line). Pick the residuals and the Newton update among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="string-statics-newton" />

One intersection is $(0.6,0.8)$. From a start near $(0.5,0.5)$, Newton with an analytic or finite-difference Jacobian locks onto that root. The circle also meets the line elsewhere — a bad start or flipped cos sign can send you there. Always re-check residuals and geometry after convergence.

## Physical checklist (full string problem)

When you return to tensions and angles:

- All $T_i>0$ (strings do not push).
- $|s_i|,|c_i|\le 1$ after every identity replacement.
- Redraw the masses: horizontal components must cancel; verticals must support the weights.
- Prefer $c=+\sqrt{1-s^{2}}$ when the sketch needs $\cos>0$.
