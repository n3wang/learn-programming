# 6.2 Bisection search

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §6.2. Rewritten in original wording — **not** a paste of `Bisection.py`. Continues [6.1 Quantum bound states](./06-01-quantum-bound-states). Labs: [6.2b Bisection labs](./06-02b-bisection-labs).

## Interval halving

Suppose $f$ is continuous on $[x_{-},x_{+}]$. The intermediate-value theorem plus repeated discarding of the unsigned half traps a root. Pick the sign condition and the midpoint update among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="bisection-search" />

![Figure 6.1 — evaluate the midpoint; keep the half where the endpoint product stays negative.](/img/computational-physics/fig-6-1-bisection.svg)

Stop when $(x_{+}-x_{-})/2<\varepsilon$ (or $|f(x)|$ is tiny). The reported root is the final midpoint.

## Implementation notes

- Pick $\varepsilon$ from the physics (energy to $10^{-10}$ or better), not from “as small as possible.”
- Poles of $\tan$ / $\cot$ can fake sign changes — use the equivalent residual

$$
g(E)=\sqrt{E}\,\cot\sqrt{10-E}-\sqrt{10-E}
$$

and a bracket already known to have opposite signs (e.g. $[8.0,8.8]$ for $V_0=10$).
- Bisection needs only the **sign** of $f$, so mild evaluation noise is usually tolerable.

## Warm-up polynomial

Before the well, practice on

$$
h(x)=x^{3}-x-2=0,\qquad x\in[1,2].
$$

$h(1)=-2$, $h(2)=4$, so a root is trapped; bisection yields $x\approx 1.5213797068$.

## Back to the well

With $g(E)$ from §6.1 on $[8.0,8.8]$, the same loop returns

$$
E_{B}\approx 8.5927852752.
$$

Check $|g(E_{B})|$ in scientific notation — it should sit near machine round-off for a tight $\varepsilon$.
