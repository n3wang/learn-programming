# 10.5 Discrete wavelet transforms

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §10.5. Rewritten in original wording — **no** textbook `dwt.py` dump. Continues [10.4b](./10-04b-wavelet-transforms-labs). Next: [10.5b](./10-05b-dwt-labs).

## Only $N$ independent coeffs

With $N$ samples $y_m$ you can keep only $N$ independent transform numbers and still hit the sample grid. Stay consistent with $\Delta\omega\Delta t\gtrsim 2\pi$ while doing so. Pick the dyadic lattice, the orthonormal reconstruction, and the Daub4 taps among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="dwt-pyramid-daub4" />

Then $Y_{j,k}\simeq\sum_m\psi_{j,k}(t_m)\,y_m\,h$. Orthonormality $\int\psi_{j,k}^*\psi_{j',k'}=\delta_{jj'}\delta_{kk'}$ plus time localization ⇒ low correlation ⇒ good compression.

![Figure 10.8 — equal-area time–frequency tiles.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-8-tf-tiles.svg)

Low $\omega$: few wide tiles (smooth outline). High $\omega$: many narrow tiles (detail). Same area $\sim 2\pi$.

## Filters instead of integrals

Industrial DWTs use **multiresolution analysis** (Mallat): pass the signal through digital **lowpass** $L$ and **highpass** $H$ filters (convolutions = discrete wavelet overlaps), then **↓2** decimate. Recurse on the lowpass branch.

![Figure 10.9 — L/H tree with factor-of-2 decimation.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-9-filter-tree.svg)

Highpass outputs are **details** $d^{(level)}$ (store). Lowpass outputs are **smooth** $s^{(level)}$ (feed the next stage). Stop when two smooth and two detail numbers remain.

![Figure 10.10 — pyramid downsampling.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-10-pyramid.svg)

**Synthesis** reverses the arrows: upsample, apply transpose filters, merge until length $N$ returns.

## Daub4 coefficients

Daubechies filters are defined by tap weights, not closed forms. Stack $L=(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)$ and $H=(c_3,-c_2,c_1,-c_0)$ (and shifted copies) into an orthogonal matrix — the $c_i$ you picked above. $L$ smooths (weighted average); $H$ differences (detail). Larger $N$ builds a banded filter matrix with wrap-around at the edges; pyramid reorders smooth/detail after each stage.

![Figure 10.12 — Daub4 packets via inverse filtering (schematic).](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-12-daub4.svg)

Inverse-transform a unit impulse in coefficient space to **see** the time-domain Daub4 shape at different scales.

## Practice checklist

2. Stop the pyramid at different `nend` to watch ↓2 stages.
3. Invert (`sign` flip) and match $y$.
4. Impulse → mother visualizations at growing widths.
