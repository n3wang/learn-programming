# 9.4.3–9.4.4 Filters and windowed sinc

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §9.4.3–9.4.4. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [9.4b](./09-04b-noise-filtering-labs). Next: [9.4c labs](./09-05b-filters-sinc-labs).

## Convolution = filtering

A linear filter maps input $f$ to output $g$ by convolution with an impulse response $h$. In frequency space that becomes a product. An ideal brick-wall lowpass is a rect in $\omega$ whose transform is a sinc. Pick $g=f*h$, the convolution theorem, and rect$\leftrightarrow$sinc among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="convolution-filter-sinc" />

![Figure 9.4 — block diagram of a filter.](/img/computational-physics/fig-9-4-filter-block.svg)

$h(t)$ is the response to $\delta(t)$. Causality: $h(\tau)=0$ for effects before causes (no peeking into the future). Filter in frequency by **multiplying** spectra, then inverse-transform — often cheaper than a long time-domain convolution.

![Figure 9.5 — RC lowpass and highpass.](/img/computational-physics/fig-9-5-rc-filters.svg)

Analog RC sketches ($\tau=RC$) and the ideal rect$\leftrightarrow$sinc pair are part of the guided pick above: $|H_{\mathrm{lp}}|$ falls as $\omega$ grows; $|H_{\mathrm{hp}}|\to 1$ at large $\omega$ and vanishes at $\omega=0$. Phase comes from the imaginary pieces; magnitude carries the pass/stop story.

## Digital taps

A delay line with coefficients $c_n$ implements

$$
h(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N} c_n\,\delta(t-n\tau)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
H(\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{N} c_n e^{-in\omega\tau},
$$

and on a continuous (or sampled) input

$$
g(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N} c_n f(t-n\tau).
$$

Knowing the $c_n$ is knowing the filter. A DFT itself is a bank of such frequency taps.

## Windowed sinc (ideal lowpass, made practical)

![Figure 9.7 — rect in $\omega$ ↔ sinc in $t$.](/img/computational-physics/fig-9-7-rect-sinc.svg)

Discrete sinc kernel (centered, then shifted to $t\ge 0$)

$$
h[i]=\frac{\sin\bigl[2\pi\omega_c(i-M/2)\bigr]}{i-M/2},
$$

truncated to $M+1$ taps ($M$ even). Truncation $\Rightarrow$ Gibbs ringing in $H(\omega)$. Soften the cut with a **Hamming window**

$$
w[i]=0.54-0.46\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{M}\right),
$$

and use $h[i]\,w[i]$. Choose $\omega_c$ as a fraction of the sampling rate; $M$ sets how sharp the transition band is. Same tool pretreats signals before a DFT to fight aliasing (§9.3).
