# 12.2 From bits to qubits

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §12.2. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [12.1b](./12-01b-dirac-notation-labs). Next: [12.2b](./12-02b-qubits-labs).

Classical memory stores **bits** $0$ and $1$. QC stores **qubits**: normalized superpositions of $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$, manipulated by quantum gates. Hardware may be superconducting circuits (Josephson junctions), not necessarily neutral-atom levels — but the math is the same spin-½ language.

## Bloch sphere and tensor product

![Figure 12.1 — Bloch sphere.](/img/computational-physics/fig-12-1-bloch.svg)

A normalized qubit $|\psi\rangle=u|0\rangle+v|1\rangle$ ($|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=1$) is a point on the Bloch sphere. Multi-qubit states use Kronecker products. Pick the Bloch parametrization and $[a,b]^{T}\otimes[c,d]^{T}$ among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="qc-qubit-bloch" />

$\theta=0$ → north pole $|0\rangle$; $\theta=\pi$ → south pole $|1\rangle$; generic pure states live on the sphere’s surface. Two-qubit computational basis: $|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$ as the standard $4$-D unit vectors.

## Takeaway

1. Qubit = normalized $u|0\rangle+v|1\rangle$.
2. Bloch angles $(\theta,\phi)$ picture pure states.
3. $n$ qubits live in $\mathbb{C}^{2^n}$ via $\otimes$.
