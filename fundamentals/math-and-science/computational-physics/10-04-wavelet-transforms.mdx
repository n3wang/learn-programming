# 10.4 Wavelet transforms

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §10.4. Rewritten in original wording — **no** textbook `cwt.py` dump. Continues [10.3b](./10-03b-stft-labs). Next: [10.4b](./10-04b-wavelet-transforms-labs).

## Definition

Replace the STFT’s eternal complex exponential by a localized wavelet packet. Pick the CWT integral and the daughter formula among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="cwt-scale-tau" />

$Y(s,\tau)$ is the overlap of $y$ with a wavelet of **scale** $s$ centered at **time** $\tau$. Scale maps to frequency as $\omega=2\pi/s$: small $s$ $\Leftrightarrow$ high $\omega$ $\Leftrightarrow$ fine time detail.

## Mothers and daughters

Start from a mother, e.g. $\Psi(t)=\sin(8t)\,e^{-t^{2}/2}$, and build the daughters you picked above. Explicitly,

$$
\psi_{s,\tau}(t)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sin\!\left[\frac{8(t-\tau)}{s}\right]
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(t-\tau)^{2}}{2s^{2}}\right].
$$

![Figure 10.4 — scale and translate an oscillating Gaussian mother.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-4-daughter-wavelets.svg)

$s&lt;1$ squeezes the packet (higher frequency); $s&gt;1$ stretches it; $\tau$ slides the center. The $1/\sqrt{s}$ factor equalizes power across scales (other normalizations exist). An inverse integrates over $\tau$ and $s&gt;0$ with a mother-dependent constant $C$ (finite $(s,\tau)$ grids reconstruct up to edge effects).

![Figure 10.5 — slide $\tau$ at fine $s$, then coarsen $s$ and repeat.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-5-cwt-scan.svg)

## Mother requirements

1. Real-valued $\Psi$.
2. Zero mean: $\int\Psi=0$ (must oscillate).
3. Localized / square-integrable packet.
4. Vanishing moments $\int t^{k}\Psi=0$ for $k=0,\ldots,p-1$ — kills smooth polynomials so the transform highlights **detail**.

Self-similarity across scales is why fractal language shows up later (Chapter 14).

## Continuous wavelet transform (CWT) practice

Implement mothers (Morlet, Mexican hat, Haar), form daughters, and numerically integrate (10.14) on a $(s,\tau)$ grid for:

- pure / multi-tone sines,
- the staged signal (10.1),
- a half-wave.

Invert approximately and compare to $y$ (normalize). Expect near-overlap except near edges.

![Figure 10.6 — input vs reconstructed overlap.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-6-cwt-reconstruct.svg)

![Figure 10.7 — scalogram idea for staged tones.](/img/computational-physics/fig-10-7-cwt-scalogram.svg)

For the staged multi-tone, a Morlet CWT scalogram should show **one** dominant band early, then a second (smaller $s$), then a third — time-resolved where a global DFT is not.

Discrete / dyadic choices of $(s,\tau)$ come in §10.5. Prefer library CWTs for production after one slow reference integrator.
