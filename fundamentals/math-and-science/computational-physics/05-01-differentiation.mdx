# 5.1 Differentiation algorithms

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), Ch. 5 intro–§5.1.2. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [4.3b RNG test labs](./04-03b-random-test-labs). Labs: [5.1b Differentiation labs](./05-01b-differentiation-labs).

This chapter opens with **numerical differentiation** — short but foundational — then spends most of its length on integration algorithms, ending with Monte Carlo integration (a different species). Here we only take the differentiation half through central differences.

## 5.1 The problem

Figure 5.1 is the spirit of the task: a projectile path with air resistance, measured only at discrete times. You need $dy/dt$ as a function of $t$, and there is **no** closed formula to differentiate by hand.

The calculus limit $dy/dt=\lim_{h\to 0}[y(t+h)-y(t)]/h$ is hostile on a machine. As $h$ shrinks, $y(t+h)-y(t)$ rides the edge of machine epsilon $\varepsilon_m$ (subtractive cancellation), while dividing by a vanishing $h$ invites overflow. You need stable finite-$h$ formulas and an eye for the truncation / round-off tradeoff from Chapter 3.

## Forward and central differences

Taylor-expand one step ahead, rearrange into a finite-$h$ slope, then compare the **forward** chord $(t,t+h)$ with the **central** chord $(t-h/2,t+h/2)$. Walk the definition → rearrange → fd → cd — pick the matching equation at each step (lookalikes included).

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="forward-central-diff" />

Geometrically, forward difference is the slope of the chord from $t$ to $t+h$ (Figure 5.1 left); truncation is typically **$O(h)$**. Central difference cancels even powers of $h$, leaving leading error **$O(h^{2})$** when $y$ is smooth.

**Parabola check.** For $y=a+bt^{2}$, the exact derivative is $2bt$, while forward difference returns $2bt+bh$ (good only when $h\ll 1/|b|$). Central difference returns the **exact** $2bt$ for every $h$ (higher derivatives vanish).
