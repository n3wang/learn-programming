# 6.4 Magnetization search

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §6.4. Rewritten in original wording. Continues [6.3 Newton–Raphson search](./06-03-newton-raphson). Labs: [6.4b Magnetization labs](./06-04b-magnetization-labs).

## Problem: $M(T)$ for a mean-field magnet

$N$ spin-$1/2$ moments $\mu$ sit in an effective field and thermalize at temperature $T$. Boltzmann weights favor alignment with the field, so more spins occupy the lower level $N_L$ than the upper level $N_U$. The net magnetization is

$$
M=\mu\,(N_L-N_U).
$$

In the Weiss molecular-field picture the dominant field is proportional to $M$ itself ($B=\lambda M$), which closes into a transcendental relation with $M$ on both sides. Strip it to reduced variables and a residual — pick each equation among lookalikes.

<GuidedChoiceExplanation preset="weiss-mean-field-reduce" />

As $T\to 0$, $\tanh\to 1$ and $M\to N\mu$ (full alignment). $m=0$ always solves the reduced equation; spontaneous order $0<m(t)\le 1$ exists only for $t<1$. Sweeping $t$ and root-finding $f(m,t_i)=0$ builds $m(t)$.

![Figure 6.4 — $f(m,t)$ vs $m$ at three temperatures; nontrivial zero only for $t&lt;1$ (below $T_c$).](/img/computational-physics/fig-6-4-magnetization.svg)

## Searching strategy

Reuse the tools from §6.2–6.3:

1. At fixed $t=0.5$, bracket the nontrivial zero (e.g. $[0.5,1]$ — $f$ changes sign) and bisect.
2. Start Newton near that root (e.g. $m_0=0.9$) with a forward-difference $f'$.
3. Compare iteration counts; then tabulate $m(t)$ on a grid $t\in(0,1]$.

Six significant figures for $t=0.5$ give

$$
m(0.5)\approx 0.957504.
$$
