# 9.5 Fast Fourier transform

> **Source:** Ideas from *Computational Physics* (Landau, Páez, Bordeianu), §9.5. Rewritten in original wording — no textbook code dumps. Continues [9.4c](./09-05b-filters-sinc-labs). Next: [9.5b](./09-06b-fft-labs).

## Why $N^2$ hurts

The DFT

$$
Y_n=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} Z^{nk} y_k,\qquad Z=e^{-2\pi i/N}
$$

needs a complex multiply for each $(n,k)$ pair — about $N^2$ work. At $N\sim10^3$ that is the difference between a coffee break and an overnight job. Cooley–Tukey (and earlier rediscoveries) reuse the **periodicity of $Z^{nk}$** to cut the cost to roughly $N\log_2 N$.

## Divide, butterfly, conquer

Split the data into halves, transform each half, combine. Each stage of length $N$ costs $O(N)$ butterflies; $\log_2 N$ stages $\Rightarrow O(N\log N)$. Pick the butterfly map $(y_p,y_q)\mapsto(y_p\pm Z y_q)$ and the bit-reversal consequence among lookalikes.

For $N=8$, powers of $Z$ collapse to four independent values ($Z^0..Z^3$ and sign flips). Grouping $y_p\pm y_q$ before multiplying by $Z$ removes redundant work — 24 complex multiplies in a staged butterfly network versus 64 in the naive sum.

## Bit reversal

With $b=\log_2 N$ bits, reverse the bits of each index $0..N-1$ (table below for $N=8$).

| dec | bits ($N=8$) | reversed | dec′ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 000 | 000 | 0 |
| 1 | 001 | 100 | 4 |
| 2 | 010 | 010 | 2 |
| 3 | 011 | 110 | 6 |
| 4 | 100 | 001 | 1 |
| 5 | 101 | 101 | 5 |
| 6 | 110 | 011 | 3 |
| 7 | 111 | 111 | 7 |

Same idea with 4 bits for $N=16$ (first half of reversed indices are even). Production FFTs hide this shuffle; you should still know it exists when comparing hand butterflies to library `fft`.

## Practice stance

Prefer a trusted FFT for production spectra. Implement a tiny radix-2 butterfly / bit-reverse once so the $N\log N$ story is visceral — then call libraries. Pair with §9.4 filters: FFT → multiply by $H(\omega)$ → iFFT is the fast convolution route.
