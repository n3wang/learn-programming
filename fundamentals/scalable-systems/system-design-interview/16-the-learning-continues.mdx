# The Learning Continues

> **Adapted from:** Alex Xu, *System Design Interview – An Insider's Guide* (2020), Chapter 16 — reorganized and annotated for this course.

Good system design comes from years of accumulated knowledge. The fastest shortcut is to study **real architectures**: how actual companies solved actual scaling problems. As you read, look for two things:

- **Shared principles.** The same ideas keep recurring: caching, replication, sharding, queues, denormalization, and fanout.
- **The underlying technologies.** For each tool a write-up mentions, ask what problem it solves and why it was chosen over the alternatives.

The original source materials sometimes used short links (`goo.gl`). Below, each one has been replaced with its **resolved destination**, and each reading is tagged with the lesson in this course that it extends.

---

## How to Read an Architecture Write-Up

Apply the same [four-step framework](./a-framework-for-system-design-interviews) you'd use in an interview, in reverse:

1. **Scope**: what problem and scale were they solving (users, QPS, data size)?
2. **High-level design**: what are the main components and data flows?
3. **Deep dive**: which one or two components were the hard part, and what trade-off did they choose?
4. **Wrap-up**: what broke later, and what would they change? Real write-ups are most valuable on this point.

Keep a running list of **"problem → technique"** pairs. For example: hot keys → consistent hashing plus replication of hot data. Celebrity fanout → hybrid push/pull. Stale caches → lease-based invalidation. That list is your toolbox in interviews.
