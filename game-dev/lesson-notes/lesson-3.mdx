# Understanding players

## 3.1 Using SCAMPER on your favourite game

:::caution Instructor question
Have Sharpies and Post-it notes ready (paper and pens work too).

Choose one of your favourite games. Select **two mechanics** (ideally the main ones) and pass each through **at least 4** SCAMPER lenses: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse.

For this session, **do not judge your ideas**. Let every idea out, even ones you think are bad. More ideas are better. You may repeat the same lens; it still counts as one lens.
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
Put a time limit on. About 5–10 minutes per mechanic with a timer. Time pressure helps creativity.
:::

## 3.2 Brainstorming new mechanics

:::caution Instructor question
Before you start, have Sharpies and Post-its. Follow the video: timer (5–10 minutes is fine), a relaxed space, and if you use music, prefer repetitive patterns without lyrics.

Define the **goal** of the session. You might want a mechanic that improves combat (movement, defence, and so on), or a **gameplay experience** (strategy, push-your-luck). Focus the session on that.

When you have enough ideas by **count** (not quality), pick three you like (or three at random), run them through SCAMPER, and note variations. You are brainstorming on top of brainstorming.

Then pick the **three** most interesting ideas, write them below with a brief description each. **Do not playtest yet.**
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
This activity is about the **number** of ideas, not the quality.
:::

:::info Reflection opportunity
How different was this brainstorming session compared with 2.3.2?

## 3.3 Playtest your new mechanics

:::caution Instructor question
You should have between 1 and 3 mechanics to add. Judge them by playing. Grab another person (this is a 2-player game). Even better: two people play while you watch, analyse, and take notes.

To get the most from the session:

- **Focus on one aspect of the game.** Do not try to test everything. Analyse one aspect: a pair of mechanics (attack and life; attack and mana), player perception (easy to learn, hard to master; fairness), or similar.
- **Note your desired player experience.** What should players feel? Use that as a lens when you analyse success.
- **Ask questions after play.** Do they remember how attacking works, or the best way to win? Sticky design shows up in what players remember.
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
Give players the assignment rules plus any mechanic changes so far. Answer questions **before** they start. If they ask while playing, ask: “How do you think that works?” After you hear them, you may correct them if it blocks your test goals — or let them play “wrong” and ask why they prefer that over your intended experience.
:::

:::info Reflection opportunity
How did the session go? Could you test your goals? Do mechanics need to change? What was the hardest part of running a playtest?
:::
