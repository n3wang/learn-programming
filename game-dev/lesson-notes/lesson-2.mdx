# Game design basics

## 2.1 Asteroids elements

:::caution Instructor question
Play the classic arcade-era game **Asteroids**. While you play (or after), note every element of the game you can find and add a short description for each. Play as long as you need. This is analysis, not memory.

When you finish listing elements, unpause the course video.

If a provided link does not work, search for “play asteroids game”.
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
The word “elements” is deliberately vague. List what *you* consider the elements of the game. If you are unsure, look up the word “elements” and then try the task.
:::

## 2.2 What is the role for you?

:::caution Instructor question
Think about the different game designer roles described in the video. Choose one or two that caught your attention and look on job sites for that role. LinkedIn or Indeed work well; a web search can help too.

Focus on **role responsibilities** (what you would do) and the **experience** or **skills** needed.

Then answer:

- What **experience** do you already have? What are you lacking right now?
- Which **skills** could you improve, and how?
- Would you feel comfortable carrying the **responsibilities** in the job description?
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
If you cannot find an open job for a specific role, broaden the search (for example, another country). Or visit a favourite game company and see what they are hiring for.
:::

## 2.3.1 List all the mechanics in Tiny Pixel Fights

:::caution Instructor question
Take Tiny Pixel Fights and make a bulleted list of **all mechanics** at this stage. Mechanics are the verbs players can engage in. Define each in a few paragraphs.
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
If you struggle to find mechanics, think: “The players can _____ and _____ and _____.” Those blanks are likely the mechanics.
:::

:::info Reflection opportunity
How much time do you think this activity would take for a AAA game? Or a small indie game?
:::

## 2.3.2 Modify and/or add a new mechanic

:::caution Instructor question
Take **one** mechanic from 2.3.1 and modify it **in any way you want** (do not hunt for the “perfectly fun” change). Write the modified mechanic, then test how the game feels (that is why you change only one at a time).

After that, **add a new mechanic** too. If you do not know where to start, play a [reference game from Lesson 1](./lesson-1) and steal an idea that seems cool here.

When you finish, write a bulleted list of **all** mechanics with a few paragraphs describing each.
:::

:::tip Gabriel’s tip
Playtesting is one of the best ways to see if a new or modified mechanic works. You will go deeper in the next lesson; try a play now and notice how different it feels.
:::
